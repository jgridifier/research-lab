Optimal transport on kernels: learning guide

Markov–Wasserstein kernels, Bayesian foundations, and post-training augmentation invariance.

Critical terminology warning. In this research line, “kernel” means a Markov kernel (a measurable map from inputs to probability measures), not an RKHS / Mercer / Gram kernel. Optimal transport enters by putting a Wasserstein metric on the codomain.
Basics: Markov kernel vs RKHS kernel

A Markov kernel is a map x↦K(x)x\mapsto K(x)x↦K(x) that returns a probability distribution for each input xxx (a conditional law). Example: x↦N(f(x),σ2)x\mapsto\mathcal{N}(f(x),\sigma^2)x↦N(f(x),σ2). This is not the “kernel” of kernel methods (Mercer / Gram / RKHS kernels, which are similarity functions k(x,x′)k(x,x')k(x,x′)). On this page “kernel” always means the Markov sense unless otherwise noted.

Basics: Wasserstein-ppp and couplings

A coupling of two probability measures μ\muμ and ν\nuν is any joint π\piπ whose marginals are μ\muμ and ν\nuν. The Wasserstein-ppp distance is the cheapest way (in LpL^pLp cost) to move mass from μ\muμ to ν\nuν:

Wp(μ,ν)=(inf⁡π∈Π(μ,ν)∫dp(x,y) π(dx,dy))1/p.W_p(\mu,\nu)=\Bigl(\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int d^p(x,y)\,\pi(dx,dy)\Bigr)^{1/p}.Wp​(μ,ν)=(π∈Π(μ,ν)inf​∫dp(x,y)π(dx,dy))1/p.

Intuition: think of μ\muμ as a pile of dirt and ν\nuν as a hole; WpW_pWp​ is the least work to fill the hole. The space of measures with finite ppp-moments, written Pp\mathcal{P}_pPp​, equipped with WpW_pWp​, is itself a metric space. When p=1p=1p=1 this is closely related to the Earth Mover’s Distance.

One-screen brief and research arc

What the area is. A bridge between classical Bayesian / statistical inference and modern generative / representation learning, built by treating distribution-valued maps as first-class objects and putting metrics from optimal transport on them.

Research arc (thematic, not biographical).

  1. Foundations (2023): Markov kernels valued in Wasserstein spaces; integration and disintegration; Bayesian inverse maps; generalized Bayes law; proved convergence of the expected update under stated hypotheses (Proposition 5.7 and Theorem 5.10). Bayesian Inference for Markov Kernels Valued in Wasserstein Spaces, PhD dissertation, Arizona State University, 2023. Dissertation (2023-10-24 public copy, 57 pp.) read for this revision via the addendum.
    Basics: Disintegration

    Any joint law γ\gammaγ on X×YX\times YX×Y can be written as a marginal on XXX times a family of conditionals on YYY given xxx (or the other way around). That family of conditionals is a Markov kernel. Disintegration is the measure-theoretic version of “conditioning.” The dissertation builds an integration theory so this statement sits inside an LpL^pLp/Wasserstein calculus, not only a σ\sigmaσ-algebra calculus.

  2. unverified Latent-space OT dependence (2024 talk): Sliced Wasserstein dependence as a training objective for autoencoders; Bayesian reading of what latent statistics mean after decoding (SIAM Conference on Mathematics of Data Science, 2024 abstract).
    Basics: Sliced Wasserstein

    Full WpW_pWp​ in high dimension is costly. Sliced Wasserstein averages one-dimensional WpW_pWp​ after projecting both measures onto many random lines. It is a Monte Carlo approximation used for training objectives such as Wasserstein correlation (WaCo) in the TMLR paper. It is not the same as Sinkhorn (entropic OT), though both are common computational shortcuts.

  3. Post-training invariance (2025–2026): Augmented encoders formalized as Markov–Wasserstein kernels; two OT-based losses (Markov–Wasserstein / MaWa minimization; Wasserstein correlation / WaCo maximization) that add augmentation invariance to a frozen pretrained encoder via a tiny adapter, without wrecking clean-feature geometry. Paper: Post-Training Augmentation Invariance, arXiv:2505.11702 (v3 dated 2026-06-08), published in Transactions on Machine Learning Research (2026); OpenReview forum id Z4uUwU6zRe. Earlier arXiv title/version: Invariant Representations via Wasserstein Correlation Maximization.
    Basics: Markov–Wasserstein metric

    Once each value K(x)K(x)K(x) lives in a Wasserstein space, two kernels KKK and LLL can be compared by averaging Wp(K(x),L(x))W_p(K(x),L(x))Wp​(K(x),L(x)) over the input measure μ\muμ:

    MWp(K,L)=(∫XWp(K(x),L(x))p μ(dx))1/p.\mathrm{MW}_p(K,L)=\Bigl(\int_X W_p\bigl(K(x),L(x)\bigr)^p\,\mu(dx)\Bigr)^{1/p}.MWp​(K,L)=(∫X​Wp​(K(x),L(x))pμ(dx))1/p.

    So MW is “Wasserstein, then LpL^pLp over xxx.” It measures how different two conditional-distribution-valued maps are, not how different two pushforward laws are.

Three-sentence summary of “OT on kernels”. A Markov kernel is a map x↦K(x)∈P(Y)x \mapsto K(x) \in \mathcal{P}(Y)x↦K(x)∈P(Y) (a conditional distribution). Putting the Wasserstein metric WpW_pWp​ on Pp(Y)\mathcal{P}_p(Y)Pp​(Y) and an LpL^pLp-type integral metric on the space of such maps yields Markov–Wasserstein kernels, which support both structural (categorical / Bayesian) and quantitative (convergence, approximation) questions. Downstream ML work uses the same objects to define augmented encoders and OT-based losses that enforce augmentation invariance while approximately preserving metric structure of pretrained features.

Deep dive — optimal transport on Markov kernels

B.1 Intuition first

Function approximation vs distribution approximation. The recorded talk opens by contrasting:

  • Function approximation: given pairs (xi,yi)(x_i, y_i)(xi​,yi​), find f:X→Yf: X \to Yf:X→Y. Methods (harmonic analysis, RKHS kernels, GPs, nets) differ, but the problem statement is uniform.

  • Distribution approximation: parameter estimation for PDEs, fitting mixture models, probabilistic programming (distributions as data structures), generative models (learn a law on images so you can sample / condition). Problems look less unified.

Unifying move. Study distribution-valued functions F:X→P(Y)F: X \to \mathcal{P}(Y)F:X→P(Y) — i.e. Markov kernels — but equip XXX, P(Y)\mathcal{P}(Y)P(Y), and the space of kernels with metrics so one can ask quantitative questions (rates, stability, convergence), not only measure-theoretic ones. Optimal transport supplies the metric on probability measures.

What this is not. It is not “OT between RKHS kernel matrices,” not MMD-as-primary-object (though MMD appears as a contrast in the later paper’s HSIC experiments), and not Sinkhorn training of couplings as the main algorithm in the 2023 talk. Sinkhorn / entropic OT appear in the broader OT-ML literature the paper cites; the core objects here are Wasserstein geometry on the value space of Markov kernels.

Markov–Wasserstein kernel. Specialize YYY to a Wasserstein space Pp(Z)\mathcal{P}_p(Z)Pp​(Z) with dY=Wpd_Y = W_pdY​=Wp​. Elements of LμXp(X,Pp(Z))L^p_{\mu_X}\big(X,\mathcal{P}_p(Z)\big)LμX​p​(X,Pp​(Z)) are Markov–Wasserstein kernels. The metric is written MWp\mathrm{MW}_pMWp​:

Interpretation: distance between two conditional-distribution-valued maps, averaged in LpL^pLp over the input measure.

Talk claim (foundations). These objects form a “generalized distribution-valued LpL^pLp space,” convenient for distribution-approximation problems in Bayesian inference, generative modeling, and probabilistic programming. Categorical structure (probability monads / monad structure) connects to PPL semantics; metrics enable classical statistical questions simultaneously.

Basics: Monad (plain language)

A monad packages “a type of object” together with a way to wrap a point into that type (here: turn a point into a Dirac measure) and a way to flatten nested instances (here: integrate a measure of measures into a single measure). Theorem 4.10 says the Wasserstein spaces (Pp,δ,m)(\mathcal{P}_p,\delta,m)(Pp​,δ,m) form such a structure on complete separable metric spaces—the ppp-Kantorovich monad. You only need the slogan: it is the categorical language for “probability measures with a notion of distance.” It does not by itself give Bayesian inversion, because inverses depend on the prior.

B.5 Wasserstein dependence and Wasserstein correlation (ML paper)

Wasserstein dependence (Post-Training…, Def. 2.1), order ppp:

WDp(π)=Wp, dXY(π, π1⊗π2),π∈Pp(X×Y).\mathrm{WD}_p(\pi) = W_{p,\,d_{XY}}\big(\pi,\ \pi^1\otimes\pi^2\big), \quad \pi\in\mathcal{P}_p(X\times Y).WDp​(π)=Wp,dXY​​(π, π1⊗π2),π∈Pp​(X×Y).

OT analog of mutual information’s DKL(π ∥ π1⊗π2)D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi\,\|\,\pi^1\otimes\pi^2)DKL​(π∥π1⊗π2), but with WpW_pWp​ instead of KL.

Wasserstein correlation (Def. 2.2) — normalized to avoid trivial “spread latent codes to infinity”:

WCp(π)=WDp(π)(WDp(πD1)⋅WDp(πD2))1/p,\mathrm{WC}_p(\pi) = \frac{\mathrm{WD}_p(\pi)}{\big(\mathrm{WD}_p(\pi^1_D)\cdot\mathrm{WD}_p(\pi^2_D)\big)^{1/p}},WCp​(π)=(WDp​(πD1​)⋅WDp​(πD2​))1/pWDp​(π)​,

where πD1\pi^1_DπD1​ is the diagonal coupling of the first marginal with itself (self-variance), similarly πD2\pi^2_DπD2​. Bounded in [0,1][0,1][0,1] under the paper’s setup.

Sliced versions SDp\mathrm{SD}_pSDp​, SCp\mathrm{SC}_pSCp​: replace WpW_pWp​ by SWpSW_pSWp​. Empirical estimate: joint empirical measure of pairs (xi,Eθ(xi))(x_i, E_\theta(x_i))(xi​,Eθ​(xi​)); approximate product by coordinate shuffling (paper cites prior screening work (2023) for the shuffle trick).

Remark (paper Remark 3.5). WaCo maximization alone is a poor generic InfoMax stand-in: it pushes toward approximate (local) isometries / structure preservation, which prevents the semantic clustering contrastive methods want — and that is exactly why it suits post-training invariance (do not scramble a pretrained latent geometry).

B.6 Augmented encoders (the ML “kernel” object of study)

Augmentation. Measurable t:A×X→Xt: A\times X\to Xt:A×X→X with parameter law ν\nuν on AAA. Collections T={tj}T=\{t_j\}T={tj​}, weights wjw_jwj​, parameter laws νj\nu_jνj​.

Augmented encoder (paper Eq. 10). For deterministic encoder E:X→ZE: X\to ZE:X→Z,

ET(x)=∑j=1mwj∫AjδE(tj(a,x)) νj(da)∈Pp(Z).E^T(x) = \sum_{j=1}^{m} w_j \int_{A_j} \delta_{E(t_j(a,x))}\,\nu_j(da) \in \mathcal{P}_p(Z).ET(x)=j=1∑m​wj​∫Aj​​δE(tj​(a,x))​νj​(da)∈Pp​(Z).

Empirically: for each xix_ixi​, draw sss augmented codes (paper often uses s=3s=3s=3) and form a discrete mixture. This is explicitly a Markov–Wasserstein kernel X→Pp(Z)X\to\mathcal{P}_p(Z)X→Pp​(Z).

Collision-aware invariance. Exact invariance E(t(a,x))=E(x)E(t(a,x))=E(x)E(t(a,x))=E(x) for all aaa can force representational collapse when augmentations create collisions across distinct data points. Paper defines unique / collision-free augmentations UAugμX(x,t)\mathrm{UAug}_{\mu_X}(x,t)UAugμX​​(x,t) and (t,μX)(t,\mu_X)(t,μX​)-invariance only on those (Defs. 3.1–3.3).

Post-training target (Def. 3.4). Given frozen pretrained F:X→ZF: X\to ZF:X→Z and admissible maps V\mathcal{V}V (e.g. isometries), an adapter E:Z→WE: Z\to WE:Z→W is (t,μX,F,V)(t,\mu_X,F,V)(t,μX​,F,V)-invariant if E∘FE\circ FE∘F is (t,μX)(t,\mu_X)(t,μX​)-invariant and (E∘F)(x)=(V∘F)(x)(E\circ F)(x)=(V\circ F)(x)(E∘F)(x)=(V∘F)(x) on supp(μX)\mathrm{supp}(\mu_X)supp(μX​) for some V∈VV\in\mathcal{V}V∈V. Goal: add invariants without corrupting the clean latent point cloud.

Collision rate (Def. 3.2). Fraction of augmented features whose nearest clean neighbor in latent space has a different class label. After rigid alignment (orthogonal + translation), residual collision rate predicts whether MaWa optimization is feasible (low → success; high, as on supervised ResNet50 in the paper → MaWa collapse).

B.7 Algorithms / losses

Algorithm A — Markov–Wasserstein (MaWa) minimization / anchored MSE

Practical batch loss (Eq. 21). Freeze FFF. Train Eθ:Z→ZE_\theta: Z\to ZEθ​:Z→Z. For batch {xi}i=1N\{x_i\}_{i=1}^N{xi​}i=1N​ and sss augments aika_i^kaik​ per point:

LMaWa(θ)=1N∑i=1N[1s+1∥(Eθ∘F)(xi)−F(xi)∥22+1s+1∑k=1s∥(Eθ∘F)(t(aik,xi))−F(xi)∥22].\begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MaWa}}(\theta) &= \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N\Bigg[ \frac{1}{s+1}\big\|(E_\theta\circ F)(x_i)-F(x_i)\big\|_2^2 \\ &\qquad + \frac{1}{s+1}\sum_{k=1}^{s}\big\|(E_\theta\circ F)(t(a_i^k,x_i))-F(x_i)\big\|_2^2 \Bigg]. \end{aligned}LMaWa​(θ)​=N1​i=1∑N​[s+11​​(Eθ​∘F)(xi​)−F(xi​)​22​+s+11​k=1∑s​​(Eθ​∘F)(t(aik​,xi​))−F(xi​)​22​].​

  • Term 1: anchor — stay near identity on clean features.

  • Term 2: invariance — pull augmented features toward the clean feature of the same xix_ixi​ (not toward each other in a collapse-prone way).

OT derivation (Eqs. 22–25). Include identity as a trivial augmentation with weights (1s+1,ss+1)(\frac{1}{s+1},\frac{s}{s+1})(s+11​,s+1s​). Then

θ∗=arg⁡min⁡θ MW22((Eθ∘F)T, δF),\theta_\ast = \arg\min_\theta\ \mathrm{MW}_2^2\big((E_\theta\circ F)^T,\ \delta_F\big),θ∗​=argθmin​ MW22​((Eθ​∘F)T, δF​),

and uniqueness of couplings to Diracs reduces W22W_2^2W22​ pointwise to squared Euclidean terms, recovering the batch loss.

When it works. Low aligned collision rate in FFF’s latent space (self-supervised backbones in the paper). Fails / collapses when residual collisions are high (supervised ResNet50 example in the paper).

Algorithm B — Wasserstein correlation (WaCo) maximization

Post-training form (Eq. 27):

θ∗=arg⁡max⁡θ WCp ⁣(∫X(δF(x)⊗(Eθ∘F)T(x)) μX(dx)).\theta_\ast = \arg\max_\theta\ \mathrm{WC}_p\!\left( \int_X \big(\delta_{F(x)}\otimes (E_\theta\circ F)^T(x)\big)\,\mu_X(dx) \right).θ∗​=argθmax​ WCp​(∫X​(δF(x)​⊗(Eθ​∘F)T(x))μX​(dx)).

From-scratch / dim-reduction form (Eq. 26): replace F(x)F(x)F(x) by xxx and allow Eθ:X→ZE_\theta: X\to ZEθ​:X→Z with ZZZ lower-dimensional.

Practice: maximize sliced correlation SC2\mathrm{SC}_2SC2​ on the empirical joint that mixes clean and augmented pairs (Eqs. 28–29). Optional AE reconstruction upper-bounds WpW_pWp​ by an L2L^2L2 decode term (Eqs. 30–32).

Properties vs MaWa. Competitive invariance; supports dimensionality reduction; more robust when MaWa’s identity-anchoring fights high collision rates; looser isometry (lower R2R^2R2 of distance–distance scatter than MaWa in reported tables).

Baselines that fail the post-training goal (empirically in the paper)

  • SimCLR-style contrastive loss on adapter outputs: can improve some invariance numbers but corrupts pairwise distance structure (R2R^2R2 near 0; huge NRMSD).

  • HSIC maximization (kernel dependence in the RKHS sense): similarly corrupts geometry; included precisely as an alternative dependence measure.

B.10 What it buys you / limitations (from paper §5)

Buys.

  • Add task-relevant invariants (rotation, noise, affine, crop) without fine-tuning foundation weights.

  • Preserve clean geometry → downstream probes on clean data stay strong.

  • Tiny adapters (1-hidden MLP) suffice when collision structure cooperates.

  • Conceptual unity: same MW-kernel language for Bayesian foundations and SSL adapters.

Limits.

  • Sample efficiency / convergence slows as augmentation parameter dimension grows.

  • MaWa structurally limited by high residual collision rates.

  • WaCo theory still thin (paper leaves rigorous sliced-vs-full WC maximization justification to future work).

  • Composite vs separate augmentations: training on {t1,…,tm}\{t_1,\dots,t_m\}{t1​,…,tm​} separately need not yield invariance to t2∘t1t_2\circ t_1t2​∘t1​ unless composites are included.

  • Expected-update convergence is proved in Proposition 5.7 and Theorem 5.10; Theorem 5.10 gives no rate, and exact updates and recovery questions remain open (§6.1).

Glossary

  • Markov kernel. Measurable map K:X→P(Y)K: X\to\mathcal{P}(Y)K:X→P(Y) (conditional distributions).

  • Wasserstein space. (Pp(Y),Wp)(\mathcal{P}_p(Y), W_p)(Pp​(Y),Wp​).

  • Markov–Wasserstein (MW) kernel. Markov kernel viewed as an LpL^pLp map into a Wasserstein space; metric MWp\mathrm{MW}_pMWp​.

  • Markov–Lebesgue integral. Defs. 3.1–3.2 integrate countably simple kernels as ∑jμ(Ej)νj\sum_j\mu(E_j)\nu_j∑j​μ(Ej​)νj​. Def. 3.4 specifies weakly Cauchy sequences; Theorem 3.9 supplies uniform-WpW_pWp​, weakly Cauchy approximations. Def. 3.11 takes their integral limit; Theorem 3.12 identifies it with iterated integrals. See B.4 for formulas.

  • Disintegration. Theorem 3.16: an LpL^pLp joint law equals Markov–Lebesgue integrals of conditional kernels against either marginal, literally; see B.4 for the equality.

  • Bayesian inverse map. Def. 5.1: the prior-dependent inverse is a disintegration of the joint law; §6.1.1 defines an approximate inverse by small integrated WppW_p^pWpp​ error. See B.4 for formulas.

  • Wasserstein dependence WDp\mathrm{WD}_pWDp​. WpW_pWp​ distance from a joint to the product of its marginals.

  • Wasserstein correlation WCp\mathrm{WC}_pWCp​. Variance-normalized dependence in [0,1][0,1][0,1].

  • Sliced Wasserstein / SC. Monte Carlo projections used for tractable WaCo training.

  • Augmented encoder ETE^TET. Mixture/kernel of encodings of random augmentations of xxx.

  • Post-training augmentation invariance. Add invariants with frozen FFF via adapter EθE_\thetaEθ​ while preserving clean latent geometry up to admissible VVV.

  • MaWa loss. Anchored MSE = empirical MW22\mathrm{MW}_2^2MW22​ to δF\delta_FδF​.

  • WaCo loss. Maximize (sliced) WC of the joint induced by the augmented encoder.

  • Collision rate. Frequency that an augmented feature’s nearest clean neighbor has another class; aligned version after rigid registration.

  • HSIC. RKHS dependence criterion used as a failing baseline for the post-training geometry goal.

  • Sinkhorn distances. Entropic OT couplings (related literature; not the MaWa closed form).
