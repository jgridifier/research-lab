§0. One-screen brief

Why OT matters for planners. Planning and risk work already lives in distributions (demand scenarios, P&L distributions, forecast fans, return densities). OT gives three practical things:

  1. A geometry for distributions that respects "how far mass must move" — a forecast off by 5 units is closer than one off by 50, unlike KL or total variation, which saturate once supports stop overlapping.

  2. A robustness language (Wasserstein distributionally robust optimization, DRO): optimize against every distribution within distance ε\varepsilonε of the data. For many problems this becomes a convex program — often a regularized version of what you already run.

  3. Cheap 1-D closed forms: in one dimension OT is sorting/quantiles. W2W_2W2​ averaging = quantile averaging; distribution regression = regression on quantile functions. Most planning variables are 1-D or low-D.

§1. Wasserstein DRO for decisions High

Core idea. You have NNN observations ξ^1,…,ξ^N\hat\xi_1,\dots,\hat\xi_Nξ^​1​,…,ξ^​N​ of an uncertain quantity (demand, renewable output, returns). Sample average approximation (SAA) optimizes against the empirical law P^N\hat P_NP^N​ and overfits when NNN is small. Wasserstein DRO hedges against every law within WpW_pWp​-distance ε\varepsilonε:

min⁡x sup⁡Q: Wp(Q,P^N)≤ε EQ[ℓ(x,ξ)].\min_x\ \sup_{Q:\,W_p(Q,\hat P_N)\le\varepsilon}\ \mathbb E_Q[\ell(x,\xi)].xmin​ Q:Wp​(Q,P^N​)≤εsup​ EQ​[ℓ(x,ξ)].
Basics: SAA (sample average approximation)

Glossary (§14). SAA — sample average approximation: optimize against the empirical distribution.

Key dual (type-1, strong duality).

sup⁡W1(Q,P^N)≤εEQ[ℓ]=inf⁡λ≥0{λε+1N∑i=1Nsup⁡ξ∈Ξ[ℓ(x,ξ)−λ∥ξ−ξ^i∥]}.\begin{aligned}&\sup_{W_1(Q,\hat P_N)\le\varepsilon}\mathbb E_Q[\ell]\\&=\inf_{\lambda\ge0}\Big\{\lambda\varepsilon+\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\sup_{\xi\in\Xi}\big[\ell(x,\xi)-\lambda\|\xi-\hat\xi_i\|\big]\Big\}.\end{aligned}​W1​(Q,P^N​)≤εsup​EQ​[ℓ]=λ≥0inf​{λε+N1​i=1∑N​ξ∈Ξsup​[ℓ(x,ξ)−λ∥ξ−ξ^​i​∥]}.​

For piecewise-linear losses and polyhedral support this is a linear program (background paper below). If ℓ(x,⋅)\ell(x,\cdot)ℓ(x,⋅) is LLL-Lipschitz and the adversary has room to move mass, the worst case is EP^N[ℓ]+Lε\mathbb E_{\hat P_N}[\ell]+L\varepsilonEP^N​​[ℓ]+Lε — the decision does not change, only the certified cost does (our worked example reproduces this exactly). Type-2 (W2W_2W2​) balls, or binding support bounds, are what actually move decisions.

Regularization view. For linear/logistic models, Wasserstein DRO reproduces norm-regularized estimators (square-root lasso, regularized logistic regression), with the ball radius playing the role of the penalty and giving it a statistical meaning.

§2. Mean–covariance (Gelbrich) robust risk — the standout result High (standout)

Core idea. Practitioners trust means and covariances more than full return distributions. Put a ball in (mean, covariance) space using the Gelbrich distance (= W2W_2W2​ between Gaussians):

G((μ1,Σ1),(μ2,Σ2))=∥μ1−μ2∥2+tr⁡[Σ1+Σ2−2(Σ21/2Σ1Σ21/2)1/2].\begin{aligned}&\mathbb G\big((\mu_1,\Sigma_1),(\mu_2,\Sigma_2)\big)\\&=\sqrt{\|\mu_1-\mu_2\|^2+\operatorname{tr}\big[\Sigma_1+\Sigma_2-2(\Sigma_2^{1/2}\Sigma_1\Sigma_2^{1/2})^{1/2}\big]}.\end{aligned}​G((μ1​,Σ1​),(μ2​,Σ2​))=∥μ1​−μ2​∥2+tr[Σ1​+Σ2​−2(Σ21/2​Σ1​Σ21/2​)1/2]​.​

Key result (Mean-covariance robust risk measurement — arXiv 2021, v3 2025 — arXiv:2112.09959; full text read). For any law-invariant, translation-invariant, positively homogeneous risk measure (VaR, CVaR, spectral, distortion, mean–std) and a "stable" structural set (all distributions, symmetric, log-concave, Gaussian…), the worst-case risk of portfolio www over the Gelbrich ball of radius ρ\rhoρ is in closed form:

sup⁡Q∈Gρ(μ^,Σ^)RQ(−w⊤ξ)=−μ^⊤w+αw⊤Σ^w+ρ1+α2 ∥w∥2.\sup_{Q\in\mathcal G_\rho(\hat\mu,\hat\Sigma)}\mathcal R_Q(-w^\top\xi)=-\hat\mu^\top w+\alpha\sqrt{w^\top\hat\Sigma w}+\rho\sqrt{1+\alpha^2}\,\|w\|_2 .Q∈Gρ​(μ^​,Σ^)sup​RQ​(−w⊤ξ)=−μ^​⊤w+αw⊤Σ^w​+ρ1+α2​∥w∥2​.

  • α\alphaα is a scalar depending only on the risk measure and structure. From the paper: CVaR or VaR at tail level β\betaβ with no structure, α=(1−β)/β\alpha=\sqrt{(1-\beta)/\beta}α=(1−β)/β​; CVaR under Gaussianity, α=ϕ(Φ−1(1−β))/β\alpha=\phi(\Phi^{-1}(1-\beta))/\betaα=ϕ(Φ−1(1−β))/β; mean–std with aversion β\betaβ, α=β\alpha=\betaα=β.

  • So robust CVaR/VaR portfolio choice = Markowitz + an explicit ℓ2\ell_2ℓ2​ penalty whose weight is tied to a statistical radius (a second-order cone program).

  • As ρ→∞\rho\to\inftyρ→∞ the optimum tends to the equal-weight portfolio.

  • With sample mean/covariance of sub-Gaussian returns, ρ=(c1+c2log⁡(1/η))/N\rho=(c_1+c_2\log(1/\eta))/\sqrt Nρ=(c1​+c2​log(1/η))/N​ gives a (1−η)(1-\eta)(1−η) confidence ball — a dimension-free rate.

  • The paper gives the worst-case mean and covariance in closed form — ready-made stress scenarios for a given portfolio.

  • Gelbrich risk upper-bounds the corresponding Wasserstein-ball risk, with equality for Gaussian reference laws.

§3. Distributional regression and density time series in Wasserstein space High

Core idea. Treat a whole distribution (income distribution, cross-section of returns, intraday load profile, distribution of store sales) as the response — or even the predictor. In 1-D, W2W_2W2​ space is isometric to a convex subset of L2(0,1)L^2(0,1)L2(0,1) via the quantile function, so you regress or autoregress quantile functions and project back onto non-decreasing functions.

§13. Is anything truly "amazing"?

  • Yes — one result. The Gelbrich mean–covariance closed form (§2): one formula turns any of VaR/CVaR/spectral/mean-std into a robust, convex, regularized portfolio rule with a dimension-free 1/N1/\sqrt N1/N​ radius, equal weights as the limit, and closed-form worst-case moments for stress reporting. Rare combination of rigor and immediate usability.

  • Quietly excellent. 1-D quantile averaging as forecast combination (§4) and distributional synthetic controls (§5): simple, interpretable, hard to beat as baselines.

  • Promising but early. OT-based multivariate conformal prediction (2025, §6).

  • Not amazing on its own. Type-1 WWW-DRO for 1-D inventory: our example shows decisions often equal SAA and gains are small; value is the certificate.

  • Neural exceptions. None needed. The only neural mentions are flagged EXCEPTION (a neural dual in one MOT paper; neural-embedding entity alignment) and are excluded from recommendations.

§14. Glossary

  • Coupling / transport plan π\piπ — a joint distribution with given marginals; "who sends how much mass where".

  • Wasserstein distance WpW_pWp​ — minimum average cost (distancep^pp) of moving one distribution onto another.

  • Quantile function F−1F^{-1}F−1 — inverse CDF; in 1-D, W2W_2W2​ = L2L^2L2 distance between quantile functions.

  • Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality — W1W_1W1​ equals the largest mean difference over 1-Lipschitz functions.

  • Entropic OT / Sinkhorn — OT plus an entropy penalty; solved by alternating scaling; fast and smooth.

  • Sinkhorn divergence — debiased entropic OT cost; a valid discrepancy between distributions.

  • Sliced Wasserstein — average of 1-D Wasserstein distances over random projections.

  • Barycenter — Fréchet mean of distributions under W2W_2W2​; in 1-D, average quantiles.

  • Linear pool — mixture of forecast densities (averaging probabilities, not quantiles).

  • Gelbrich distance — W2W_2W2​ between Gaussians with given means/covariances; lower bound on W2W_2W2​ for general laws with those moments.

  • SAA — sample average approximation: optimize against the empirical distribution.

  • DRO — distributionally robust optimization: optimize against the worst distribution in an ambiguity set.

  • Ambiguity set / ball radius ε,ρ\varepsilon,\rhoε,ρ — set of plausible distributions; radius sets conservatism.

  • Type-1 / type-2 WDRO — ambiguity balls using W1W_1W1​ (Lipschitz-like regularization) or W2W_2W2​ (variance-like regularization).

  • Certificate — the worst-case objective value; an upper bound that holds with stated confidence when the radius is calibrated.

  • CVaR — expected loss in the worst β\betaβ fraction of outcomes.

  • Distortion risk measure — risk measure computed by reweighting quantiles with a distortion function (VaR, CVaR are special cases).

  • Fréchet regression — regression where the response is a metric-space object (e.g. a distribution).

  • Tangent space / log map — linearization of W2W_2W2​ space around a reference distribution; allows linear models.

  • Center-outward quantile/rank — multivariate quantile defined by the OT map to the uniform law on the unit ball.

  • Conformal prediction — calibration method giving finite-sample coverage for prediction sets.

  • Scenario reduction — replacing many scenarios by few weighted ones close in a probability metric.

  • Nested distance / adapted Wasserstein — OT distance between stochastic processes respecting information flow.

  • Martingale OT — OT restricted to martingale couplings; model-free price bounds.

  • Gromov–Wasserstein — OT comparing internal distance structures of two datasets in different spaces.

  • CRPS — continuous ranked probability score; for 1-D forecasts it equals the integrated pinball loss.

  • Markov kernel — a map from inputs to probability distributions; the object studied on the companion page.
