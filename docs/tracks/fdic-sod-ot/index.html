FDIC branch deposits × OT: Killed at burn-in (negative result)

Status: killed at burn-in. No out-of-sample run. The burn-in median next-year W₂² was 0.0078, below the half-sample noise floor of 0.0196 (ratio 0.399), and it was below the floor in every one of the 9 burn-in target years. By the pre-registered rule the object is killed: from one year to the next, the spread of branch deposits inside a state moved less than two random halves of the same state-year differ. The shape-only part of the move was also below its own noise floor. Separately, the planned test had power 0.07 with the 22 out-of-sample years available. Nothing was scored and no replacement object is proposed.

What was going to be forecast

Each year, the spread of branch deposits inside each US state (FDIC Summary of Deposits): for each state and year, the equal-weighted distribution of ln(branch deposits) minus ln(the national median branch deposit), over branches other than main offices with positive deposits, in the 50 states and DC. Two optimal-transport methods were pre-registered against simple baselines: a pooled Wasserstein autoregression around the yearly barycenter, and a Wasserstein regression on state deposit growth. The plain-language explanation is on the learning page.

The half-sample distance is the noise floor: split a state's branches in one year at random into two halves and measure how far apart their quantile functions are. If consecutive years are closer than that, the year-to-year signal is below the noise and there is nothing for a forecast to beat. Any one of the pre-stated kill rules was enough; K1 and K3 both fired. Power is a second, independent reason not to proceed: with about 22 test years and a common year shock across states, a 5% gain would be detected about 7% of the time.
