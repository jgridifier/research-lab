1. Why this paper cluster

Two mature literatures sit next to each other:

  • Dynamic factor models (DFMs) for large panels and ragged-edge nowcasting (data arrive at different lags).
  • Sinkhorn / entropic optimal transport as a differentiable loss between probability measures (batch distributions, residual clouds, feature maps).

The natural research question: can a Sinkhorn divergence replace or augment the usual Gaussian / MSE observation criterion inside a factor state-space model, so forecasts respect distributional structure while still exploiting low-rank co-movement?

Honest map: On arXiv-first retrieval, the true intersection is thin. Most hits are adjacent (deep TS + Sinkhorn, Wasserstein tensors, COT-GANs, martingale OT in derivatives). The clearest intersection paper found in this test is journal-side: Spelta, Pagnottoni & Pecora (JRSS-A 2025).

2. What original work found

Classical DFM forecasting

  • Stock & Watson: few latent factors summarize panels; forecast via estimated factors.
  • Banbura, Giannone & Reichlin: nowcasting with mixed-frequency / ragged-edge data via state-space DFMs and Kalman filtering/smoothing; EM for parameters. Observation equation \(y_t = \Lambda f_t + e_t\), factor dynamics (VAR), missing entries handled by Kalman with time-varying observation selection; typically Gaussian / MSE loss.

Sinkhorn divergence technology

  • Cuturi (2013): entropic regularization makes OT differentiable/scalable via Sinkhorn–Knopp matrix scaling.
  • Genevay / Feydy-style Sinkhorn divergence: debiased Sinkhorn loss \(S_\varepsilon(\mu,\nu) = OT_\varepsilon(\mu,\nu) - \tfrac12 OT_\varepsilon(\mu,\mu) - \tfrac12 OT_\varepsilon(\nu,\nu)\), usable as a training criterion between empirical measures.

True intersection: Spelta et al. (JRSS-A 2025)

Spelta, Pagnottoni & Pecora — Short-term forecasting with optimal transport, JRSS-A 2025 (DOI 10.1093/jrsssa/qnaf036). They (i) propose a Sinkhorn-divergence loss treating two batches from the same dataset as sharing a distribution, for filling missing low-frequency entries, and (ii) add that Sinkhorn step as a complement to the EM algorithm of a Dynamic Factor Model, with Monte Carlo and US GDP nowcasting evidence. This paper was not in the arXiv priority brief (paywalled journal; no arXiv abs in cached hits) — it was found via Jev Ultrafast on the landing page.

4. Research recipe: DFM + Sinkhorn observation loss

Goal: Improve ragged-edge multi-series short-horizon forecasts by replacing or augmenting Gaussian observation likelihood with a Sinkhorn divergence between predicted and observed panel distributions, following the Spelta-style "distribution-preserving imputation" idea.

Assumptions

  1. Approximate low-rank factor structure: \(y_t = \Lambda f_t + e_t\), \(f_t\) VAR / state-space.
  2. Missingness is ragged-edge / publication lag, treated as missing-at-random conditional on the information set at forecast origin \(t_0\).
  3. Sinkhorn acts on empirical measures over cross-section and/or residual batches.
  4. Entropic regularization \(\varepsilon\) chosen for stability; debiasing (Sinkhorn divergence) preferred.

Protocol (leakage-safe)

  • Walk-forward: expanding or rolling origin; use only data available at each origin.
  • No same-period leakage: never train or evaluate with contemporaneous realizations that arrive after the decision time.
  • Imputation vs prediction: if Sinkhorn fills missing entries inside EM (Spelta-like), freeze filled values for out-of-sample scoring; score point (RMSE/MAE) and distributional (Sinkhorn / CRPS) metrics separately.

Candidate nulls / baselines

  1. Classical Gaussian DFM + Kalman (MSE) — Banbura/Giannone/Reichlin-style.
  2. Same DFM with Student-t / Huber observation loss (robustness without OT).
  3. Sinkhorn-only nonparametric imputer without factors (Spelta stand-alone arm).
  4. Factor model + MSE, then Sinkhorn only as evaluation metric (not train).
  5. Feature-aligned N-BEATS + Sinkhorn (2305.15196) on the same panel.

Success criteria

  • Statistically better short-horizon scores vs baseline (1) on held-out origins, with Diebold–Mariano.
  • Gains concentrated where raggedness is severe (ablate missingness rate).
  • Ablation: remove Sinkhorn step → performance collapses toward MSE-DFM.

5. Top citations

  1. Stock & Watson — dynamic factor / diffusion-index forecasting (classical DFM).
  2. Banbura, Giannone & Reichlin — DFM nowcasting with ragged edge / Kalman.
  3. Cuturi (2013) + Genevay/Feydy Sinkhorn divergence — OT loss technology.
  4. Spelta, Pagnottoni & Pecora (JRSS-A 2025) — DFM-EM + Sinkhorn loss nowcasting. DOI 10.1093/jrsssa/qnaf036.
  5. Lee et al. 2305.15196 — strongest adjacent forecasting+Sinkhorn deep baseline.

6. Open questions

  1. Is Sinkhorn best as an EM augmentation (Spelta), a direct observation loss in gradient-based state-space learning, or only an evaluation metric?
  2. How to define the ground metric on heterogeneous financial panels (scales, signs, mixed frequencies)?
  3. Computational scaling: Sinkhorn per EM iteration vs large \(N\) panels — when does it dominate Kalman cost?
  4. Does distributional matching help tail events / crises, or only average nowcast RMSE?
  5. Identification: can Sinkhorn over-smooth factor dynamics and hurt multi-step forecasts?
  6. Replication: open re-implementation of Spelta DFM+Sinkhorn on a generic public panel (e.g. FRED-MD style) under strict vintage rules.
