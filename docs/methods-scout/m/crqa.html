The statistics

The diagonal recurrence profile is the match rate along each diagonal of \(R\):

$$\mathrm{DRP}(k) = \frac{1}{n-|k|}\sum_{t} \mathbf 1[a_t = b_{t+k}] .$$

A peak at \(k>0\) means series \(a\) leads. \(rr_0 = \mathrm{DRP}(0)\) is the same-week match rate. DET is the share of recurrent points that lie on diagonal runs of length ≥ 2 (runs of matching). Anti-recurrence replaces \(b\) with \(\tilde b\).

Why it works, and the trap we hit

Step 1 · chance levelIf \(a\) and \(b\) were unrelated, \(P(a_t = b_{t+k}) = \sum_s \hat p^a_s \hat p^b_s\). With quintiles each \(\hat p_s = 0.2\), so chance is \(5 \times 0.2^2 = 0.20\). An observed same-week rate of 0.287 is 44% above chance.
Step 2 · the full-plot rate ignores time orderThe default summary is \(\mathrm{RR} = \frac1{n^2}\sum_{i,j}\mathbf 1[a_i=b_j]\). Group by state: \(\sum_{i,j}\mathbf 1[a_i=b_j] = \sum_s \#\{i:a_i=s\}\,\#\{j:b_j=s\} = n^2\sum_s \hat p^a_s\hat p^b_s\). It depends only on how often each state occurs, not on when, so shifting \(b\) cannot change it: our first run got p = 1 in all 96 windows. Only order-aware statistics (\(\mathrm{DRP}(k)\), \(rr_0\), DET) can be tested by shifting.
Step 3 · why states, not correlationQuintile states are unchanged by any monotone transform, and an outlier is just "state 5". The question becomes "do they visit the same (or opposite) states at the same time?", which also gives a readable sentence: high fails with a low spread.

Inference: circular-shift null with familywise control

Shift \(b\) circularly by \(\tau \ge 26\) weeks (12 months monthly), 999 times: \(b^{(\tau)}_t = b_{(t+\tau) \bmod n}\). Each series keeps its own persistence and state shares; only the alignment breaks. For lags, use the maximum over the lag window, \(T = \max_{|k|\le 13}\mathrm{DRP}(k)\), and compare it with the null maxima: a single familywise p-value for "a link at some lag". The lead/lag asymmetry test compares \(\sum_{k>0}\) with \(\sum_{k<0}\) of the profile under the same null.

Toy example (computed on the box)

\(a = (1,2,2,3,1)\), \(b = (2,2,3,1,1)\). Same week (\(k=0\)): matches at \(t=2\) and \(t=5\), so \(\mathrm{DRP}(0) = 2/5\). One step (\(k = -1\), compare \(a_t\) with \(b_{t-1}\)): 4 of 4 match, \(\mathrm{DRP}(-1) = 1\). At \(k=+1\): 0/4. The profile peaks at \(k=-1\): \(b\) leads \(a\) by one step (\(a\) copies \(b\)). Note that \(\mathrm{RR}\) for these two series would not change if we shuffled \(b\): same state counts.

How it differs from the standard tool. A lagged Spearman correlation measures linear-in-ranks association across the whole range. CRQA counts co-visits of discrete states, so it is blind to magnitude within a state but can say which states line up, and it windows naturally. On these data the lag profile mostly agreed with the rank correlation (ρ = −0.35 at lag 0); the additions are the state reading, a clean familywise lag test, and the rolling on/off picture.

Followed: Coco & Dale (2014), Frontiers in Psychology 5:510 (categorical CRQA, diagonal recurrence profile); Coco, Mønster, Leonardi, Dale & Wallot (2021), The R Journal 13(1):112–130 (crqa package). Section and equation numbers not checked. Package crqa 2.1.0 (drpfromts). Toy: cards/_toys/toys.py.

Inputs and preprocessing

  • Weekly panel, 432 complete weeks (2018-06-06 to 2026-09-23), rebuilt from the lab's copies of public files: Treasury ex-TIPS dealer financing fails to deliver (PDFTD-USTET), on-the-run 5y + 10y fails, OFR DVP and tri-party volumes and rates.
  • Monthly: Form PF tab 8.27 (Question 43 creditor types, $ billions); 3-month log growth because the circular shift is rough on trending series.
  • Each series converted to quintile states (local quintiles within each 52-week window for the rolling analysis).

What we ran

  1. Weekly lag profiles ±13 weeks, co and anti modes, for fails vs spread, on-the-run fails vs spread, fails vs DVP growth, plus a sanity pair (on-the-run vs financing fails). 999 shifts each.
  2. Rolling 52-week windows (4-week step, 96 windows): \(rr_0\) and DET with 199 within-window shifts. (The first attempt used full-plot RR and was degenerate; kept for the record, not used.)
  3. Monthly profiles ±6 months for Form PF prime-broker and "reverse repo" borrowings vs repo volumes, fails and the spread.
  4. Planted 3-step lead check to confirm the sign convention.

What it means for a decision-maker

Weeks with many Treasury settlement fails were weeks when the DVP repo rate sat low relative to tri-party, from 2019 to mid-2025, strongest through mid-2022. That link was absent during the late-2025 fails spike: the usual relationship did not hold in that episode. Separately, hedge-fund "reverse repo" borrowing growth in Form PF ran about four months ahead of DVP volume growth, though Form PF arrives too late to use as an early signal. Descriptive only; not a trading signal.

Where it breaks (seen in our runs)

  • The degenerate-RR trap (Math, Step 2): full-plot RR cannot be tested by shifting. Now documented.
  • Quintiles discard magnitude: a big and a small fail spike in the top quintile count the same.
  • Circular shift wraps the end of a series onto its start; rough for trends (hence growth rates monthly).
  • Overlapping windows: the 96 tests are not independent; counts are descriptive.
  • Speed: crqa builds the full \(n\times n\) plot (8.4 s at n = 4,000); the weekly nulls took 143 s on 8 workers.

What we'd do next

Top pick (Math): an order-aware test built on \(rr_0\), the lag profile and a lead–lag contrast, with max-T control over lags and windows, against AR-matched or stationary-bootstrap surrogates instead of the wrap-around shift. Then the joint "stress-state" recurrence across all four Q1 legs.

1 · Engineering · Effort S
A Rust lag-profile engine with the shift null built in

The categorical lag profile is \(O(n\cdot L)\), but crqa builds the full \(n\times n\) plot each call. A Rust core with a Python binding would run the 999-shift null in one pass, with crqa 2.1.0 as the oracle.

Math sketch. One-hot encode states, \(A_{ts}=\mathbf 1[a_t=s]\); then \(\mathrm{DRP}(k) = \frac{1}{n-|k|}\sum_s\sum_t A_{ts}B_{t+k,s}\), a sum of per-state cross-correlations, computable for all shifts at once by FFT.

Test. Exact parity (difference 0) with drpfromts on all 8 weekly and 12 monthly profiles, and the full weekly null in < 1 s (R today: 143 s on 8 workers).

2 · Math · Effort M · ★ Top pick
An order-aware CRQA test with familywise lag control and persistence-matched surrogates

Full-plot RR is order-blind and DET barely moves. Build statistics that depend on alignment (\(rr_0\), the lag profile, a lead–lag contrast), with max-T control over lags and windows, and a stationary-bootstrap or AR-matched null instead of a wrap-around shift.

Math sketch. \(T = \max_{w,k} \big(\mathrm{DRP}_w(k) - \mu_{w,k}\big)/\sigma_{w,k}\) over windows \(w\) and lags \(k\), with \(\mu,\sigma\) from surrogates that match each series' persistence; reject where the observed statistic exceeds the 95th percentile of the surrogate \(T\).

Test. Size 5% ± 1.5 points on 2,000 simulated independent AR(1) pairs at our weekly persistence; power ≥ 0.8 for a planted same-week coupling of the 2021–22 size. The circular shift is the baseline to beat.

3 · New use on our data · Effort M
Joint "stress-state" recurrence across all four Q1 legs

Multidimensional CRQA on top-quintile stress states of fails, the spread, Form PF repo growth and CFTC TFF leveraged-fund positioning (Q1.5): do the legs co-visit stress in March 2020, March 2023 and late 2025, or come apart?

Math sketch. Joint recurrence \(J_t = \prod_{\ell=1}^4 \mathbf 1[\text{leg } \ell \text{ in top quintile at } t]\); episode statistic \(\sum_{t\in E} J_t\) vs a block-shift null per leg.

Test. A pre-registered episode list, with joint recurrence in each episode against a block-shift null at p < 0.05. An honest miss counts.
