Assumptions

  • Each indicator has an agreed direction (more drawdown, lower pre-tax income, more losses = worse). Nothing is assumed about their relative importance or scale.
  • Dominance is read exactly: a 0.1 pp difference counts as much as 5 pp. Measurement noise in the projections is ignored (the Math avenue fixes this).
  • For rank probabilities, all linear extensions are treated as equally likely (the "uniform over consistent rankings" convention of Brüggemann and of Schoch 2018). It is a reference measure, not a model of how the Fed or a bank weighs indicators.
  • For the tests: under the null, payouts are exchangeable across firms within a test year.

The estimator and the statistic

Rank probabilities and expected rank over all rankings that respect dominance:

$$P(\mathrm{rk}(u)=k)=\frac{\big|\{\ell\in\mathcal L(\preceq):\ell(u)=k\}\big|}{|\mathcal L(\preceq)|},\qquad \rho(u)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}k\,P(\mathrm{rk}(u)=k).$$

They are computed exactly on the lattice of ideals. With \(e(D)\) the number of linear extensions of the sub-poset \(D\) (ways to build \(D\) from the bottom) and \(\bar e(D)\) the number of ways to finish from \(D\) to \(V\):

$$|\{\ell:\ell(u)=k\}|=\sum_{\substack{D\in\mathcal I,\ |D|=k-1\\ u\notin D,\ D\cup\{u\}\in\mathcal I}} e(D)\;\bar e(D\cup\{u\}).$$

The null and multiple testing

  • Null: within each test year, payouts are exchangeable across firms (the dominance structure is kept fixed). 4,999 shuffles for \(C_{\mathrm{dom}}\) pooled over years (one-sided); 1,999 for the trade-off agreements (two-sided about 0.5); 999 for the SCB-vs-path comparison.
  • Holm over the five orderings in the trade-off analysis (equal-weight, in-sample OLS, and the three single indicators).
  • Same firm pairs recur across years; the within-year shuffle is valid for "no association in any year" but the pooled p-values are not firm-clustered. Year-to-year stability is reported descriptively.
Toy example (computed by cards/_toys/toys.py)

Four firms, two indicators (higher = worse): A = (1, 1), B = (2, 3), C = (3, 2), D = (4, 4). Dominance pairs: A≺B, A≺C, A≺D, B≺D, C≺D; B and C are a trade-off (B worse on indicator 2, C on indicator 1). The ideals are ∅, {A}, {A,B}, {A,C}, {A,B,C}, {A,B,C,D} (6), and there are 2 linear extensions, ABCD and ACBD. Expected ranks: A 1, B 2.5, C 2.5, D 4; P(rank of B = 2) = 0.5. With payouts A 3.0, B 1.0, C 2.0, D 0.5, dominance concordance is 1.0 (5 of 5). On the trade-off pair, indicator 2 agrees (B is worse and pays less) and indicator 1 does not (C is worse but pays more): exactly the pattern that sets pre-tax income against loan losses on our data.

How it differs from the standard tool. The usual approach builds one composite (equal weights, PC1, or a regression) and correlates its ranks with payouts. That assumes the indicators are substitutable at fixed rates and turns every trade-off into an order the analyst chose. The partial order assumes only the direction of each indicator. It keeps "not comparable" as an answer, prices the ambiguity (rank intervals and rank probabilities), and splits the evidence into a weight-free part (dominance pairs) and a part where we can ask which indicator payouts follow. A composite can report a weaker average correlation; it cannot show that two of its inputs pull in opposite directions.

What we found

Finding 1

Where one firm is worse on all three, it pays out less 72% of the time.

Across 13 severely adverse exercises (2013–2025, 360 firm-years), 2,203 within-year pairs are ordered by dominance (worse CET1 drawdown, worse pre-tax income and higher loan-loss rate together). In 71.5% of them the more-stressed firm had the lower Y-9C payout/RWA that calendar year (within-year shuffle null mean 50.0%, 95th percentile 54.7%, p = 0.0002, 4,999 shuffles); next-year payouts 65.5% (p = 0.0002).

Every positive-weight composite agrees on these pairs, so this number is the part of the stress–payout link that does not depend on weights. Payouts in calendar year Y partly precede that year's results; association only.

Finding 2

In trade-off pairs, payouts follow pre-tax income and go against loan losses.

55% of pairs per year on average (40–65%) are trade-offs: each firm is worse on something. There, the firm with worse SA pre-tax income paid less in 63.9% of 2,814 pairs (two-sided p = 0.0005); the firm with the higher loan-loss rate paid less in only 40.9% (p = 0.001, i.e. it paid more); drawdown 53.1% (p = 0.19). The equal-weight composite averages these to 55.2%.

Computed with a within-year payout shuffle (1,999). Holm over the five orderings keeps pre-tax income, loan losses, equal-weight and OLS significant. The in-sample OLS composite (61.5%) uses payouts to choose its weights, so it is optimistic. No composite can show the sign reversal: it is hidden inside the weights.

Finding 3

When the stress path and the SCB disagree, payouts follow the path.

2021–2025: in 175 pairs where one firm is worse on the whole stress path but its SCB is not higher (123 of them with both firms at the 2.5% floor), the path-worse firm paid less in 78.9% (shuffle p = 0.001). Ordering by SCB alone gets 56.8% of 1,481 pairs (p = 0.024); path dominance 71.9% of 834 (p = 0.001).

Consistent with the 11-14 IDR finding (SCB had no skill). The SCB floor hides differences that payouts still track. SCB and later payouts are linked by construction after 2020 (standardized capital actions); read descriptively.

Finding 4

Dominance relations are stable: 74% persist, 2% reverse.

For firms in consecutive tests, 1,115 of 1,553 dominance relations persist (mean share 0.74), 409 dissolve into trade-offs and 29 (1.9%) reverse. Payout-rank Kendall τ averages 0.63 and falls after 2020 (0.40–0.69 in 2020–2025 pairs vs 0.53–0.84 before).

Descriptive; no null. The stable core of the stress ordering is the part payouts track (finding 1).

Finding 5

As a predictor it loses to OLS, ARDL and SARIMAX.

Walk-forward 2015–2025 (fits use earlier years only), Spearman of predicted vs realized payout/RWA: poset expected rank 0.36, pooled OLS on the 3 stress variables 0.46, pooled ARDL(1,0) with last year's payout 0.67, per-firm SARIMAX(1,0,0)+exog 0.65 (last payout where < 8 years).

Pre-stated bar (within 0.05 of OLS with no fitted weights) failed. Payout persistence dominates any stress ranking. The method is for description, not forecasting.

What it means for a decision-maker

When one bank looks worse than another on every headline stress number, it almost always pays out less, about seven times in ten. But more than half of all bank pairs are mixed: one has bigger losses, the other weaker projected earnings. In those mixed cases payouts line up with projected earnings, and banks with higher projected loan losses actually pay out more, possibly because high-loss lenders are also high-earning card and consumer lenders. A single "stress score" blends these two and hides that they point in opposite directions. Since 2021 the capital buffer number, with many banks sitting at its 2.5% floor, says less about payouts than the full stress path does.

Where it breaks

  • Exact dominance is brittle: a 0.1 pp difference creates an order, and 26% of orders dissolve the next year. Projection noise is not modelled.
  • Loan-loss rate is per loans, pre-tax income per assets: business-model mix (card lenders, custody banks) drives the trade-off pattern. We did not control for business model.
  • Calendar-year payouts partly precede the June results; association only. After 2020, SCB and payouts are linked by construction.
  • Pooled p-values treat years as independent strata; recurring firm pairs are not clustered.
  • Exact computation scales with the number of ideals; with more indicators or hundreds of units (Q2-sized books) it would need netrankr's MCMC or approximations.
  • As a forecaster it is weak (finding 5) — payout persistence dominates.

What we'd do next

  • Noise-aware dominance (top pick): require a margin on each indicator, set from year-to-year projection variation, and recompute.
  • Repeat the trade-off scoring for dividends and buybacks separately and before/after 2020 (Q3.5).
  • Add a business-model split (card/consumer, custody, universal) to test the loan-loss sign reversal.

Well established (the computation is exact combinatorics; the applied validation literature is small)

What the home field has established

  • Origin and lineage. Ranking without weights by dominance and Hasse diagrams comes from environmental chemistry and QSAR (Brüggemann and co-authors; Springer book Ranking and Prioritization for Multi-indicator Systems: Introduction to Partial Order Applications — found via search, not read). Averaged ranks over linear extensions and the local partial-order approximation were published in J. Chem. Inf. Comput. Sci. 44(2):618 ("Estimation of averaged ranks by a local partial order model"; search snippet only). An open review of partial-order ranking alongside QSAR is at PMC2680638 (found, not read in full).
  • Network-science version. Schoch (2018, Social Networks 54:50–60; abstract and introduction read) shows many centrality indices preserve the neighbourhood-inclusion partial order, so each index is one linear extension, and proposes rank intervals, relative rank probabilities and expected ranks instead of picking an index. The search index lists 20 citations: a niche result.
  • Algorithm. Exact rank probabilities via the lattice of ideals (De Loof, De Meyer & De Baets 2006, Fundamenta Informaticae 71:309–321; De Loof's 2009 Ghent PhD thesis), cited by netrankr; not read. Counting linear extensions is #P-complete in general, so exactness depends on the poset having few ideals; netrankr's benchmark vignette (read) gives "under about 30 elements always safe" and documents MCMC (Bubley–Dyer) and approximations with their errors on 3,000 random partial rankings.
  • Software. netrankr 2.0.0 on CRAN (David Schoch; C++ via Rcpp; pkgdown site with reference pages and four vignettes; GitHub schochastics/netrankr). Maintained.
  • Critiques and failure modes. The uniform measure over linear extensions is a convention, not a model; dominance is sensitive to small, noisy differences (the chemometrics literature studies this: "On the influence of data noise and uncertainty on ordering of objects", J. Chemometrics, doi 10.1002/cem.2764 — abstract via search only); with many indicators almost every pair becomes incomparable.

1 · Engineering · Effort M
A tolerance-aware lattice engine, netrankr as oracle

Our Python lattice-of-ideals code already matches netrankr to 10−13; netrankr (C++) is faster on these sizes. The engineering value is reach, not raw speed: a Rust core with exact big-integer counts, tolerance dominance built in (avenue 2), and width-aware ordering so 60–100 firms (all CCAR participants plus IHCs over time) or a few indicators more stay exact.

Math sketch. Cost is \(O(|\mathcal I|\cdot n)\); by Dilworth, \(|\mathcal I|\le\prod_{c}(|c|+1)\) over a chain cover, so it is polynomial for fixed width \(w\): \(|\mathcal I|\le (n/w+1)^w\). Processing ideals level by level keeps memory to two levels. For width beyond about 15, fall back to Bubley–Dyer MCMC with the exact engine as a check on sub-posets.

Why it matters here. Tolerance dominance (avenue 2) increases incomparability and therefore the number of ideals; Q2-sized program books need the approximate path anyway.

Closest prior work: netrankr's C++ exact_rank_prob and mcmc_rank_prob (Schoch); De Loof et al. (2006), cited by netrankr, not read.

Test (pass bar set now). Rank probabilities within 10−12 of netrankr on all 13 years and 20 random posets; a width-12, n = 60 poset exact in under 1 s on the box; MCMC agrees with exact within 0.01 expected rank on the 13 years.

2 · Math · Effort M · ★ Top pick
Noise-aware dominance and rank probabilities

Order two firms only when one is worse by more than projection noise on every indicator; average rank probabilities over that uncertainty instead of treating 0.1 pp as decisive.

Math sketch. Replace \(\preceq\) with \(u\preceq_\varepsilon v\iff x_{vj}-x_{uj}\ge\varepsilon_j\ \forall j\) (an interval order on each indicator, so \(\preceq_\varepsilon\) is still a partial order; transitivity holds because margins add). Choose \(\varepsilon_j\) from the year-to-year standard deviation of firm-level changes in indicator \(j\) for firms whose business mix did not change. Then a "probabilistic dominance" version: with \(x_u\sim N(\hat x_u,\Sigma)\), \(P(u\prec v)\) by simulation, and rank probabilities \(\mathbb E_{x}[P(\mathrm{rk}(u)=k\mid\preceq_x)]\) by drawing posets and averaging exact results. Concordance tests use only pairs with \(P(u\prec v)\ge 0.9\).

Why it matters here. 26% of dominance relations dissolve the next year and 2% reverse; some are surely noise. If the 72% concordance is driven by robust pairs, it should rise; if it is driven by hair-thin differences, it will fall.

Closest prior work: noise and uncertainty in partial-order ranking, J. Chemometrics, doi 10.1002/cem.2764 (search snippet: a "stability range" of noise over which the Hasse diagram is unchanged); a fuzzy posetic toolbox, Annals of Operations Research (2024), doi 10.1007/s10479-024-06352-3 (search only). Neither read; no application to stress tests found.

Test (pass bar set now). On the 13 years: year-to-year kept share of dominance relations rises from 0.74 to ≥ 0.85 while ≥ 50% of the original ordered pairs remain ordered; payout concordance on robust pairs ≥ 0.70 (within-year shuffle p < 0.01). Null: the same procedure with ε applied to indicator values shuffled across firms within year.

3 · New use on our data · Effort S
Trade-off resolution for the payout mix (Q3.5)

Score trade-off pairs indicator by indicator, separately for dividends/RWA and net buybacks/RWA, before and after the 2020 SCB regime.

Math sketch. \(A_j^{(m,r)}\) = share of trade-off pairs in regime \(r\in\{2013\text{–}19,\ 2020\text{–}25\}\) where the firm worse on indicator \(j\) has the lower payout component \(m\in\{\text{div},\text{bb}\}\). Contrast \(\Delta_j=A_j^{(\text{div},r)}-A_j^{(\text{bb},r)}\); within-year shuffle that moves dividends and buybacks together (keeps their firm-level link).

Why it matters here. The 11-16 PIM run suggested the drawdown link moved from dividends to buybacks after 2020; trade-off pairs can say which indicator each payout channel follows without choosing weights.

Closest prior work: none found for stress-test payouts; method is A1b of this card.

Test (pass bar set now). At least one indicator with \(|\Delta_j|\ge 0.08\) and Holm p < 0.05 over 3 indicators × 2 regimes; otherwise reported as "payout mix follows the same indicator as the total".
