Assumptions

  • Both series are stationary within each segment (we difference the logs to get close).
  • The interaction is mostly a delay: a time shift \(\tau\) appears as a straight line in phase against frequency. PSI still gives a sign when the phase is not straight, but only the average slope.
  • Many independent segments. The jackknife needs enough of them; with 6–15 segments it is crude (see "Where it breaks").
  • One direction of influence dominates. If both series drive each other, the phase slopes cancel and PSI reads near zero.

The statistic

$$C_{xy}(f)=\frac{S_{xy}(f)}{\sqrt{S_{xx}(f)\,S_{yy}(f)}},\qquad \Psi_{xy}=\operatorname{Im}\!\Big(\sum_{f\in F} C_{xy}^{*}(f)\,C_{xy}(f+\delta f)\Big).$$

With \(C=\alpha e^{i\phi}\) each term is \(\alpha(f)\,\alpha(f+\delta f)\sin\big(\phi(f+\delta f)-\phi(f)\big)\): the tilt of the phase, weighted by how coherent the two series are at the two neighbouring frequencies. Standardized: \(z=\Psi/\widehat{\mathrm{sd}}_{\text{jackknife}}\), with \(\widehat{\mathrm{sd}}^2=\frac{K-1}{K}\sum_k(\Psi_{(k)}-\bar\Psi)^2\) and \(\Psi_{(k)}\) the index with segment \(k\) left out.

Why it works, step by step

Step 1: a delay is a phase that rises with frequency. If \(y_t=a\,x_{t-\tau}\) then \(\hat y(f)=a\,e^{-i2\pi f\tau}\hat x(f)\), so \(S_{xy}(f)\propto e^{+i2\pi f\tau}\) and the phase is \(\phi(f)=2\pi f\tau\). Its slope against \(f\) is \(2\pi\tau\): positive when \(x\) leads.
Step 2: shared instantaneous shocks have no phase. Suppose both series are mixtures of independent sources \(s_1,s_2\): \(x=m_{11}s_1+m_{12}s_2\), \(y=m_{21}s_1+m_{22}s_2\). Then \(S_{xy}(f)=m_{11}m_{21}S_{11}(f)+m_{12}m_{22}S_{22}(f)\) is a sum of real numbers times real spectra, so \(S_{xy}\) is real and \(\operatorname{Im}\,C_{xy}=0\) at every frequency. Every term of \(\Psi\) is \(\operatorname{Im}(C^*(f)C(f+\delta f))=\operatorname{Re}C(f)\operatorname{Im}C(f+\delta f)-\operatorname{Im}C(f)\operatorname{Re}C(f+\delta f)=0\). A common market shock therefore cannot create a PSI. This is the property (a) of Nolte et al. (2008), re-derived here.
Step 3: why Granger differs. Granger asks whether the past of \(x\) helps predict \(y\) beyond \(y\)'s own past, so it is not built to ignore mixing. Haufe et al. (2013; abstract read) report that Granger measures with standard significance testing detect spurious connectivity on mixed EEG signals, while PSI does not suffer from that limit because mixing leaves no imaginary coherency. Our own simulation shows the same sign of difference but a smaller size (10% against 3%).

The null or inference

The paper's rule is \(|z|>2\) with the segment jackknife. We checked its size on our sampling: 1,000 independent pairs matched to the fitted AR(p) persistence of the real series (residuals bootstrapped), and the same with a shared same-week shock. Across the four cases \(|z|>2\) rejects 0.6%–4.1% (nominal about 5%; z standard deviation 0.71–0.94): conservative. Multiple testing: Holm over the 7 pairs on the normal p-value. Observed z are descriptive only, because the independent-pair size missed the pre-stated band [3%, 8%].

A tiny worked toy

Three neighbouring frequency bins, \(|C|=(0.8,0.8,0.7)\), phase \(\phi=(0.20,0.50,0.80)\) rad (computed in cards/_toys/toys.py):

\(\operatorname{Im}(C_1^*C_2)=0.1891\), \(\operatorname{Im}(C_2^*C_3)=0.1655\), so \(\Psi=0.3546>0\): the first series leads. With the same magnitudes and phase 0 (instantaneous mixing) \(\Psi=0.0\). For a 2-step delay the phase at \(f=0.05,0.10,0.15\) is \(0.6283,1.2566,1.8850\) rad: a slope of \(2\pi\,\delta f\,\tau=0.6283\) rad per bin.

How it differs mathematically from the standard tool

Granger / VAR lead-lag assumes the series are the whole system and tests whether lagged \(x\) lowers the prediction error for \(y\); it does not remove instantaneous correlation, and it gives a p-value, not a signed size. Cross-correlation peak lag reads a lag off one correlation curve and can be set by a common factor. PSI assumes nothing about lag order, keeps only the imaginary part of coherency (the part a shared instantaneous shock cannot produce), and summarizes the sign of the phase tilt over a band. It cannot show two-way feedback.

Nolte, Ziehe, Nikulin, Schlögl, Krämer, Brismar & Müller (2008), Phys. Rev. Lett. 100:234101, arXiv:0712.2352: definition eq. (3)–(4), the weighted-slope form eq. (5) and the jackknife |z| > 2 rule, from the open arXiv text (read via search extract; the equation numbers match the extract, and the full text was not read line by line). Step 2 is our derivation.

What we found

Finding 1

No pair shows a significant lead-lag after Holm.

Seven pairs (3 weekly, 4 monthly). Smallest raw p: Form PF prime broker and fails, z = 1.81 (p = 0.070, Holm 0.49; prime broker leads, 11 segments). Weekly fails and DVP, z = -0.61; fails and tri-party, z = -0.09; DVP and tri-party, z = -1.22; prime broker and reverse repo, z = -0.28 (computed).

The size study below shows PSI is too cautious at these sample sizes, so a null here is weak evidence, not proof of no lead-lag. Pre-registered rule: observed z is descriptive only because size missed the band for independent pairs.

Finding 2

The usual Granger test flags two pairs that PSI does not, and it false-alarms more.

VAR with AIC lag: fails to tri-party p = 0.023 and reverse repo to prime broker p = 0.042 (unadjusted), where PSI gives z = -0.09 and -0.28. On 1,000 matched-persistence independent pairs shaped like weekly fails and DVP, Granger rejects in either direction 13.4% of the time (6.6% in one stated direction); PSI rejects 0.6%. With a shared same-week shock added, Granger 12.1% and PSI 3.1%. Monthly prime broker and reverse repo: Granger 6.1% (independent), 7.0% (shared shock); PSI 1.2% and 4.1% (computed).

Granger's extra rejections are mostly the two-direction test counted at 5% each. PSI's size is below the pre-stated band [3%, 8%] for independent pairs (z standard deviation 0.71): conservative, so low power.

Finding 3

Home-field check: PSI finds the planted delay and ignores mixed sources.

SYNTHETIC, 200 runs of 5,000 samples. Coupled with a 2-sample delay: PSI direction correct and |z| > 2 in 100%. Random mixture of two independent sources: PSI false significance 3.0%; Granger (AIC lag up to 8) either direction 10.0%, one direction 6.0% (computed).

The paper's 'Granger fails badly on mixtures' is milder in our setup (10%, not near 100%). Not every number in the paper's simulations was reproduced; only this class of case.

Finding 4

Direction does not hold across halves or windows.

Prime broker and tri-party volume: z = -3.11 in the first half (2014-09..2020), +0.35 in the second (5 segments each, so the first-half z is itself unreliable). Rolling windows hold only 3 weekly or 4 monthly segments: nearly every bar is grey, and the few coloured ones are not repeated in the next window.

Windows are too short for a lead-lag map by band. The 2020-02..05 stress window falls inside the first rolling window and cannot be isolated at this window size.

Finding 5

As a forecasting aid it does not beat OLS, ARDL or SARIMAX(X).

Walk-forward one-month change in log prime-broker borrowing, 84 months from 2019-01: zero-change RMSE 0.0571; OLS on lagged reverse repo 0.0558; ARDL (BIC) 0.0558; SARIMAX(1,0,1) 0.0584; with reverse repo 0.0574; PSI-gated 0.0584 (the gate never opened). Gated vs best (OLS): -4.8% (worse), Diebold-Mariano p = 0.21.

Pre-stated bar (>= 3% lower RMSE, DM p < 0.10) failed. Forecasting was low priority.

Finding 6

Engineering: a vectorised PSI is 6x faster; parity with mne-connectivity was missed.

200 channel pairs x 10,000 samples, 77 segments, with the jackknife: loop 1.26 s, vectorised 0.21 s (6.1x); z identical to 1e-15. Against mne-connectivity 0.9.0 phase_slope_index (fourier mode) our PSI differs by up to 0.27 on values near 1.5: its coherency differs from a plain-FFT coherency in the second digit under every window we tried, and we did not trace why.

Pre-registered parity bar (<= 1e-8) missed. Our own checks pass: PSI(x,y) + PSI(y,x) = 0.0 and rescaling the series changes PSI by 2e-16 (computed); the toy and the simulation recover the planted direction. mne is therefore not used as the oracle.

How to read the main chart

  1. Left picture: the usual rolling correlation, one panel per pair. It says how tightly the two move, never who leads.
  2. Right picture: one bar per window. Height = PSI (positive: the first series in the panel title leads). Blue/orange = significant at the 5% t-level for that window's number of segments; grey = not.
  3. Almost all bars are grey: with 3–4 segments per window nothing short of an enormous PSI is significant. Treat a coloured bar as a prompt, not a finding.

What it means for a decision-maker

On the public funding data we have, no series reliably runs ahead of another. The two links the standard test flagged (fails ahead of tri-party volume; reverse repo ahead of prime-broker borrowing) are what that test also reports about 6–13% of the time for pairs we know are unrelated, so they are not a basis for a trading or monitoring rule. The method itself is also cautious at our short sample sizes, so this is "no evidence", not "evidence of none".

Where it breaks

  • Short samples: 6–15 segments make the jackknife conservative (size 0.6–4.1%). Rolling windows hold only 3–4 segments.
  • First vs second half disagree (prime broker × tri-party z = −3.11 then +0.35), so even a "significant" result here would not be stable.
  • The stress window 2020-02..05 cannot be isolated: the first rolling window already spans it.
  • Two-way feedback cancels the phase slope. Lead-lag is not causal direction in nonlinear systems (a 2024 comparison with conditional mutual information, abstract read).
  • Parity with mne-connectivity was missed; see the engineering finding above.

Well established as a linear lead-lag measure; contested as proof of causal direction

(a) Robust in its home field

  • Origin and claims. Nolte et al. (2008, PRL 100:234101; arXiv:0712.2352, abstract and definition read) propose PSI as (a) insensitive to mixtures of independent sources, (b) meaningful for non-linear phase spectra, (c) properly weighted over frequencies. Their simulations: for truly unidirectional flow without noise PSI detects direction about as well as Granger causality; for random mixtures of independent sources Granger yields highly significant false results and PSI does not. An EEG application on 88 subjects (eyes closed) shows a front-to-back flow. The search tool lists about 594 citations.
  • Independent assessment. Haufe, Nikulin, Müller & Nolte (2013, NeuroImage; abstract read): in a simulation study PSI performs well under volume conduction, while Granger measures with standard significance testing produce spurious connectivity; a time-reversed-data surrogate makes Granger measures work about as well as PSI. (The authors overlap with the PSI paper.)
  • Failure modes and critiques. Nolte et al. 2010 (PMLR v6, abstract read): PSI, being linear, can miss nonlinear interactions. A 2022 review (Front. Neuroinformatics/PMC, snippet read): under two-way interaction PSI fails to describe direction. A 2024 paper (PubMed 38631615, abstract read): lead/lag from PSI is not generally equivalent to causal direction in nonlinear systems.
  • Maintenance. Implemented in mne-connectivity (phase_slope_index, v0.9.0, BSD-3, actively maintained, read its source) and the authors' MATLAB code (TU Berlin causality page). EEG/MEG use for over a decade.
  • Our reproduction. Planted-delay and mixture simulations reproduce the direction of the paper's claims (PSI 100% correct on coupled; false significance 3% vs Granger 10% on mixtures), but not the size of the Granger failure. Parity with mne-connectivity was not achieved (see On our data).

Verdict: well established for what it measures (a linear lead-lag direction, robust to instantaneous mixing); contested as "causality". Reason: independent simulation support and long use, with documented limits.

(b) Transfer with new results

  • Assumptions that break: few segments (6–15, not hundreds of epochs); series that are persistent and heavy-tailed; shared shocks that are real economic drivers rather than measurement mixing.
  • A result the standard toolkit gets wrong: on matched-persistence independent pairs shaped like weekly fails and DVP, the standard VAR Granger test rejects in either direction 13.4% of the time (nominal 5% per direction); PSI 0.6%. The two Granger "links" in the real data (p = 0.023, 0.042) sit inside that false-alarm range.
  • Further fields it could carry to: climate teleconnections (lead-lag between indices), hydrology (rainfall-runoff delay with shared storms), macro nowcasting with a common factor, ecology (predator-prey delays).

A vectorised core that returns Ψ, jackknife z and the local phase-slope spectrum for thousands of pairs at once, with parity against mne-connectivity as the oracle.

Math sketch. Keep the segment cross-spectra sums \(\sum_k X_kY_k^*\), \(\sum_k|X_k|^2\), \(\sum_k|Y_k|^2\) once; each leave-one-out \(\Psi_{(k)}\) is then a subtraction and one pass over the band, cost \(O(PKF)\) for \(P\) pairs rather than \(O(PK^2F)\).

2 · Math · Effort M · ★ Top pick
A small-sample-calibrated PSI test

Replace the segment jackknife with a delta-method standard error from the complex-Wishart coherency estimate, with a t reference on K − 1 degrees of freedom, or a time-reversal surrogate null.

Math sketch. \(\Psi\) is a smooth function of the averaged cross-spectrum matrix \(\hat S\), which for \(K\) independent segments is complex Wishart; \(\operatorname{Var}\hat\Psi\approx\nabla\Psi^\top\,\Sigma_{\hat S}\,\nabla\Psi\) with \(\Sigma_{\hat S}\) from Wishart fourth moments. The alternative null reverses time in one series' segments: instantaneous mixing is unchanged by reversal, a true delay changes sign, so \(\Psi\) under reversal is the null for pure mixing (the device used for Granger by Haufe et al. 2013).

Why it matters here. Our size study shows the jackknife z has sd 0.71–0.94 and rejects 0.6–4.1% at K = 11–15: a "no lead-lag" result carries little weight. A calibrated test would tell us whether the null on fails, DVP and prime-broker borrowing is real.

Closest prior work: Nolte et al. (2008) jackknife (read); Haufe et al. (2013) time-reversed surrogates for Granger measures (abstract read). No small-K calibration study of the PSI jackknife was found in the sources read.

Test (pass bar set now). On the matched-persistence simulations used here (weekly fails × DVP, monthly prime broker × reverse repo; independent and shared shock), size in [0.03, 0.07] for 1,000 runs each; power ≥ 0.80 for a planted 2-week (weekly) or 2-month (monthly) delay at the observed signal-to-noise; null = the independent pairs.
