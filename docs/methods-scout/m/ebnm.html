Notation

SymbolMeaningHere
\(x_j\)one noisy observation per unita program's log 2025 level (relative to its group), or its log change 2024→2025
\(s_j\)its known standard errorfrom the spread of the program's budget-minus-actual gaps
\(\theta_j\)the true valuethe program's true level or true change
\(g\)the prior: how true values are spread across unitsestimated from all programs at once
\(\mathcal G\)the family \(g\) is chosen fromnormal, point-normal, scale mixture of normals (ash), unimodal, NPMLE

The model

$$x_j \mid \theta_j \sim \mathcal N(\theta_j, s_j^2),\qquad \theta_j \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} g \in \mathcal G .$$

Step one estimates the prior by maximum marginal likelihood, integrating each \(\theta_j\) out:

$$\hat g = \arg\max_{g\in\mathcal G}\ \sum_j \log \int \mathcal N(x_j;\theta, s_j^2)\, g(d\theta).$$

Step two reports, for every program, the posterior mean \(\hat\theta_j = \mathbb E[\theta_j \mid x_j, \hat g]\), a posterior interval, and the local false sign rate

$$\mathrm{lfsr}_j = \min\{P(\theta_j \ge 0 \mid x_j,\hat g),\ P(\theta_j \le 0 \mid x_j,\hat g)\},$$

the probability that we have the direction of the change wrong.

Why it works, step by step

Step 1 · normal prior gives a closed formWith \(g = \mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2)\) the posterior is normal with mean \(\hat\theta_j = \mu + B_j (x_j-\mu)\), where \(B_j = \sigma^2/(\sigma^2+s_j^2)\). Noisy programs (large \(s_j\)) get small \(B_j\) and are pulled hard toward the group; precise ones keep their own number.
Step 2 · the data tell us σ²Marginally \(x_j \sim \mathcal N(\mu, \sigma^2 + s_j^2)\): the observed spread is true spread plus noise. With equal \(s\), the MLE is \(\hat\sigma^2 = \max\{0, \tfrac1n\sum (x_j-\bar x)^2 - s^2\}\): subtract the noise you know about from the spread you see. That is James–Stein shrinkage, derived rather than tuned.
Step 3 · flexible priors shrink adaptivelyA scale mixture \(g=\sum_k \pi_k\,\mathcal N(0,\sigma_k^2)\) on a fixed grid makes the marginal \(\sum_k \pi_k \mathcal N(x_j;0,\sigma_k^2+s_j^2)\); the log-likelihood is concave in \(\pi\), so the fit is a convex problem (solved by mixsqp). Tight groups get strong shrinkage, grab-bag groups get little, without being told which is which.
Step 4 · lfsr replaces "is it significant?"A z-test asks whether \(\theta_j = 0\) is implausible. lfsr asks the decision question, "how likely am I wrong about up vs down?", and because it uses \(\hat g\) it borrows strength: a modest move in a family where everything moves is believable.

Inference and checks

  • No null to choose and no penalty: \(\hat g\) is fitted, then posteriors follow. Calibration is checked against the answer key instead: among lfsr 0.05–0.2 calls, 11–13% of signs were wrong; 0.2–0.5, 39–40%.
  • Uncertainty in \(\hat g\) itself is ignored (plug-in empirical Bayes); with 1,000–1,700 programs this is small.
Toy example (computed on the box)

Four programs, each with \(s = 1\): \(x = (2.0,\ 0.5,\ -0.5,\ 3.0)\). Mean \(\hat\mu = 1.25\); observed spread \(1.81\); so \(\hat\sigma^2 = 1.81 - 1 = 0.81\) and \(B = 0.81/1.81 = 0.45\). Posterior means: \(1.59,\ 0.91,\ 0.47,\ 2.03\) (each pulled 55% of the way to 1.25). Posterior SD \(=\sqrt{0.81\cdot1/1.81} = 0.67\). lfsr: \(0.009,\ 0.086,\ 0.243,\ 0.001\). Only the first and last are confident "up" calls at lfsr < 0.05.

How it differs from the standard tool. Benjamini–Hochberg treats each program alone (\(z_j = x_j/s_j\)) and controls the share of false discoveries of "≠ 0". ebnm first learns what true changes look like across all programs, then judges each one against that, and reports a sign-error probability. Same data, same \(s_j\): 91 vs 14 calls on the Corning mock, with 0% wrong sign. Against complete pooling (group means) it never has to be told whether a group is a real family.

Followed: Willwerscheid, Carbonetto & Stephens (2025), JSS 114(3), §1 eqs (1)–(3) (model, prior family, posterior mean) and §2.1 eqs (8)–(10) (normal-prior MLE and posterior); lfsr from Stephens (2017), Biostatistics 18(2):275–294. Inputs and preprocessing

  • SYNTHETIC mocks (seed 20261113): Corning mock 1,079 programs FY2020–25; MS mock 1,840 programs FY2021–25. Answer key used only for scoring.
  • \(s_j\): observable spread of each program's budget-minus-actual gaps, scaled by the dataset README's stated budget/actual noise ratio, shrunk toward the group median.
  • Levels: \(x_j\) = log 2025 actual minus the mean of its stated segment × function group; shrink the deviation, add the group mean back.
  • Changes: \(x_j\) = change in log actual 2024→2025, \(s_j = \sqrt2\,\hat\sigma_j\); prior centred at 0.
  • Text check: \(x_j\) = OLS slope of log actual over a program's years (≥ 3), \(s_j = \hat\sigma_j/\sqrt{S_{xx}}\).

What it means for a decision-maker

For each program in a budget cycle you get a better estimate of where it really is, and a plain "probability we have the direction wrong". On the mocks it flags six to seven times as many genuine moves as the cautious standard test, almost never in the wrong direction, with nothing to tune. It is for programs, not totals: portfolio totals were already 98% right.

Where it breaks (seen in our runs)

  • Lone programs (no real family in their group) get slightly worse: 0.110 → 0.120 on the Corning mock. One global prior is the cause; the Math card fixes it.
  • Totals: shrinking in logs and summing in dollars moved the Corning behaviour term closer to truth ($345.5M vs $359.6M true) and the MS one further away ($5.151B vs $4.980B).
  • Text: shrinkage adds nothing over a z-score; the gain comes from peer comparison.
  • Standard errors are only as good as the budget-vs-actual noise model; on real data they must be re-derived.

1 · Engineering · Effort M
A Rust core for the ash and point-normal fits, R as oracle

A thousand programs × categories × cycles × bootstraps means thousands of fits. Port the mixture-weight solve (mixsqp's SQP) and the posterior summaries to Rust, with a thin Python binding.

Math sketch. With a fixed grid \(\sigma_1<\dots<\sigma_K\), build \(L_{jk}=\mathcal N(x_j;0,\sigma_k^2+s_j^2)\) once and maximise the concave \(\sum_j\log\sum_k \pi_k L_{jk}\) over the simplex; posteriors are then mixtures of normals with closed-form means and tail probabilities.

Test. Log-likelihood parity −145.7808 (Corning, ash) and −238.8506 (MS), posterior means within 1e-6; then wall time against R's 0.03 s per fit.

2 · Math · Effort M–L
Let the description set the prior

Make \(g\) depend on text-derived features (stated category, intent, driver), as in covariate-powered empirical Bayes. Today one global prior made lone programs slightly worse than their own number.

Math sketch. \(\theta_j \sim g_{w(c_j)}\) with mixture weights \(\pi_k(c_j) = \mathrm{softmax}(W c_j)_k\) for text features \(c_j\); fit \(W\) by marginal likelihood, cross-fitted so a program's own text does not leak (Ignatiadis & Wager, arXiv:1906.01611).

Test. On non-family programs, beat both the own number (0.110 / 0.093) and global ash (0.120 / 0.096) on both mocks, with no loss on families.
