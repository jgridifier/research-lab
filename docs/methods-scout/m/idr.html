The estimator

Score a predicted CDF \(F\) against an outcome \(y\) by

$$\mathrm{CRPS}(F,y) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big(F(z) - \mathbf 1[y\le z]\big)^2\,dz .$$

IDR picks the conditional CDFs that minimise total CRPS over the training data, subject only to stochastic order:

$$\begin{gathered}\min_{F_{x_1},\dots,F_{x_n}} \sum_{i=1}^n \mathrm{CRPS}(F_{x_i}, y_i)\\ \text{s.t. } x_i \preceq x_j \Rightarrow F_{x_i}(z) \ge F_{x_j}(z)\ \ \forall z .\end{gathered}$$

A more favourable covariate never predicts a stochastically smaller payout. No bandwidth, link, or distribution family; only the direction.

Why it works, step by step

Step 1 · CRPS splits into thresholdsThe integrand at each \(z\) only involves \(F(z)\) and \(\mathbf 1[y\le z]\). So minimising the integral means minimising, separately at every threshold \(z\), \(\sum_i (\eta_i - \mathbf 1[y_i \le z])^2\) over \(\eta_i = F_{x_i}(z)\).
Step 2 · each threshold is an isotonic regressionThe order constraint becomes "\(\eta\) is non-increasing along the order", so each threshold is a least-squares isotonic fit of a 0/1 sequence, solved exactly by pool-adjacent-violators (PAVA) in one dimension (paper §2.3, eq. 15). The objective only changes at observed \(y\) values, so a finite number of thresholds suffices.
Step 3 · the pieces fit togetherPAVA on nested indicator sequences gives values that are also monotone in \(z\), so the threshold fits stack into valid CDFs. The paper shows the solution is the same for a wide class of proper scores, not just CRPS ("universality", §2.2), and that it is calibrated.
Step 4 · skill and the nullSkill \(= 1 - \overline{\mathrm{CRPS}}_{\text{IDR}}/\overline{\mathrm{CRPS}}_{\text{clim}}\), where climatology predicts the training sample's empirical distribution for everyone. Evaluation leaves one test year out. Null: shuffle the covariate within each year 299 times and refit (smallest possible p = 1/300 ≈ 0.003).
Toy example (computed on the box)

Four firms ordered by pre-tax income, \(x = 1,2,3,4\), with payouts \(y = 0.5, 2.0, 1.0, 3.0\). At threshold \(z = 1.0\) the indicators \(\mathbf 1[y\le 1]\) are \((1, 0, 1, 0)\). The CDF must be non-increasing in \(x\), but \(0\) then \(1\) violates it, so PAVA pools firms 2 and 3: \(\hat F(1.0) = (1, 0.5, 0.5, 0)\). At \(z = 0.5\): \((1,0,0,0)\); at \(z = 2.0\): \((1,1,1,0)\). Firm 3's predictive distribution is therefore 50% at or below 1.0 and 100% at or below 2.0: a step CDF learned with no tuning.

How it differs from the standard tool. OLS with normal errors predicts a mean and a symmetric bell. Payouts are skewed and some firms issue net shares (down to −1.4% of RWA), so on the raw scale OLS scored worse than climatology (−2.2%) while IDR gained 5.8%. Quantile regression gives a few quantiles under a linear form; IDR gives every quantile under only a monotone form. The price: step functions that are coarse at our n, and a weak partial order with two or more covariates.

Followed: Henzi, Ziegel & Gneiting (2021), JRSS-B 83(5):963–993 (arXiv:1909.03725): §2.2 existence, uniqueness and universality; §2.3 eq. (15), threshold decomposition and PAVA; §3 partial orders. Package isodistrreg 0.6.0 (Rust core). Toy: cards/_toys/toys.py.

Inputs and preprocessing

  • All 643 rows of the Fed's public_results_DFAST_2026.csv, FR Y-9C payout items for 2013–2025, and 165 SCB values (effective 2021–2026), each re-verified against fresh downloads.
  • Payout = common and preferred dividends plus net buybacks (BHCK4460 + 4598 + 4783 − 4782) over RWA, calendar year \(Y\), for test year \(Y\). 360 firm-years; 18–35 banks per year.
  • Two response scales: within-year percentile (removes the payout cycle on purpose) and raw payout/RWA.

What we ran

  1. Full-sample IDR fits for drawdown (antitonic) and pre-tax income (isotonic), percentile and raw scales; OLS with normal errors on the same inputs.
  2. Leave-one-test-year-out CRPS skill vs climatology; 299 within-year shuffles per test.
  3. SCB era 2021–2025: SCB alone, drawdown alone, pre-tax alone, and SCB + drawdown under the 2-D componentwise order.
  4. Eras: fit 2013–19, score 2021–25 and vice versa; Kendall τ of each year's ranking against the prior year's.
How to read the main chart (IDR fan)
  1. x = the bank's drawdown percentile within its test year; y = its payout percentile (left) or raw payout/RWA (right). Grey dots are the 360 firm-years.
  2. The dark line is the IDR median; the bands are 25–75% and 10–90% of the predicted distribution.
  3. The steps are the estimate itself: no smoothing. A flat stretch means the data do not separate those banks.
  4. Read the band width: wide bands at a given drawdown mean the stress result says little about that bank's payout.
What it means for a decision-maker

If you want to describe how payouts line up with stress results, use projected pre-tax income under the severely adverse scenario, not the capital buffer: it explains payout ranks about four times better than the drawdown, and the SCB adds nothing. Since 2020 the link is weaker and concentrated in the worst-hit decile of banks. This is a description, not a forecast, and it does not touch the pre-registered trading study.

Where it breaks (seen in our runs)

  • Two covariates: with SCB + drawdown most pairs are incomparable, and IDR lost to OLS (5.4% vs 10.6%). This is the case for the Math card.
  • Ties at the floor: 37.9% of SCB firm-years sit at 2.5%, tying a third of the sample.
  • Dependence: firm-years within a firm are dependent; the within-year shuffle does not model that.
  • Coarse steps at n = 132–360.
  • Speed: 2-D partial order 13.2 s at n = 1,000 (1-D: 0.02 s).

Top pick (Math): learn a single monotone stress index from pre-tax income, drawdown, loan losses and SCB (a distributional single-index model), then fit IDR on that one score, with a firm-block null.

Not graded Home-field grading started with the 2026-11-16 edition. What the paper and the 11-14 page record:

  • Theory: Henzi, Ziegel & Gneiting, JRSS-B 83(5):963–993 (2021). The abstract states that IDR learns conditional distributions that are calibrated and simultaneously optimal relative to comprehensive classes of loss functions, subject to isotonicity; isotonic quantile and binary regression are special cases. §2.4 gives consistency.
  • Recommended use in its home field: the authors recommend IDR as a generic benchmark for probabilistic forecasts because it needs no tuning, and report it competitive with state-of-the-art postprocessing on precipitation forecasts (§5).
  • Follow-ons on the 11-14 watchlist: EasyUQ (SIAM Review 2024), distributional single-index models (JASA 2023), survival IDR (arXiv 2026).
  • Implementation: CRAN isodistrreg 0.6.0 (2026-08-21) with a Rust core built by Cargo.
1 · Engineering · Effort M
A Python binding to the package's own Rust core, plus a faster partial order

isodistrreg 0.6.0 already compiles a Rust core, reachable only from R. Expose it through PyO3, with R as the oracle, and fix the 2-D slowdown (13.2 s at n = 1,000).

Math sketch. In 1-D, consecutive thresholds differ by one indicator flip, so PAVA can be updated incrementally (the recursive scheme cited in §2.3); in 2-D, replace the generic QP per threshold with a minimum-lower-set algorithm on the dominance DAG.

Test. CRPS parity within 1e-10 with R on all 13 leave-one-year-out folds; 2-D fits at n = 1,000 under 1 s.

2 · Math · Effort M · ★ Top pick
A monotone stress index: one learned direction, IDR on top

With SCB + drawdown, IDR lost to OLS (5.4% vs 10.6%) because a 2-D partial order is too weak at n = 132. Learn a weighted combination of pre-tax income, drawdown, loan losses and SCB, then fit IDR on that single score.

Math sketch. \(\hat w = \arg\min_{\|w\|=1}\ \sum_{\text{folds}} \mathrm{CRPS}\big(\mathrm{IDR}(w^\top x),\, y\big)\), the distributional single-index model of Henzi, Kleger & Ziegel (JASA 2023); sign constraints on \(w\) keep the economic direction.

Test. LOYO CRPS skill above both the best single-covariate IDR (15.0% / 11.3%) and OLS on the same covariates, with a firm-block permutation p < 0.05.
