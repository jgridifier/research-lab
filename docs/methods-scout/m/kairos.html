Assumptions

  • Each type comes in and goes out of fashion once (unimodal over time: a "battleship" shape).
  • The type list is fixed across deposits.
  • Deposits are independent (used by the event model's intervals).

The statistics

Seriation by correspondence analysis. Take the SVD of the standardised residuals and order months and segments by their axis-1 coordinates:

$$S = D_r^{-1/2}\,(P - r c^{\top})\,D_c^{-1/2} = U\Sigma V^{\top},\qquad F = D_r^{-1/2}U\Sigma .$$

Event date (Bellanger & Husi): regress the known dates on the leading CA row coordinates; any mix, including a new month, then gets a date.

$$\hat t_i = \beta_0 + \sum_{k} \beta_k F_{ik}.$$

EPPM seriograph (Desachy): plot each share's excess over its column mean, \(\max(0,\ p_{ij}/r_i - \bar s_j)\), so hand-offs between types stand out.

Why it works, step by step

Step 1 · what CA finds\(S\) measures how far each month's mix is from the average mix, scaled so that \(\sum S_{ij}^2\) is the total inertia (the χ² statistic over \(n\)). Its first singular vector is the single direction of mix change that explains the most inertia, here 69.3%.
Step 2 · why a fashion cycle lands on axis 1If types rise and fall in sequence, nearby months share types and distant months share few. That gives a "horseshoe" in CA, whose first axis orders deposits along the sequence (the classical seriation result). Ordering by \(F_{i1}\) therefore recovers time up to sign: our sign is negative (Spearman −0.964).
Step 3 · dating a new monthA new mix needs no refit: its coordinate is its profile times the standard column coordinates (the CA transition formula), \(f = \sum_j (n_j/n_\cdot)\,v_{j1}/\sqrt{c_j}\). Plug \(f\) into the event model.
Step 4 · the null (Porčić)After seriation, count the modes (local peaks) in each type's sequence and sum. Under the seriation model the total is small. Compare it with 1,000 random month orders; p = share of random orders with as few modes.

Inference used for the Q1.4 link: correlation of mix speed with Form PF borrowing growth (overlapping 12-month changes), with a circular-shift null that keeps persistence, and Holm over 18 tests.

Toy example (computed on the box)

Three months, three segments, counts \(\begin{pmatrix}60&30&10\\40&40&20\\20&30&50\end{pmatrix}\). Shares by month: (.6,.3,.1), (.4,.4,.2), (.2,.3,.5); column means (.400, .333, .267).

  • EPPM: excesses are .200 for segment A in month 1, .067 for B in month 2, .233 for C in month 3, and 0 elsewhere: a clean hand-off A → B → C.
  • CA: axis 1 holds 94.7% of inertia; month coordinates −0.446, −0.106, +0.553, already in calendar order.
  • Event model: dating the months 2018, 2020, 2022 gives \(\hat t = 2020 + 3.873\,F_1\), fitted 2018.27, 2019.59, 2022.14.
  • A new mix (50, 35, 15): coordinate −0.276, so event date ≈ 2018.9: it "looks like" late 2018.
How it differs from the standard tool. The usual view is a stacked-area chart of shares, or a compositional PCA on log-ratios. Neither assumes a direction in time, so neither gives a date or a speed. Seriation adds a strong, testable structure (each type has one rise and fall) and turns the mix into a single clock. The cost: the structure can be wrong (coefficient 0.52 here, so some segments go up and down more than once), and the method is blind to a change in the type list.

Followed: Bellanger & Husi (2012), Journal of Archaeological Science 39(4):777–790, event model; Porčić (2013), Journal of Archaeological Science 40(12):4552–4559, mode-count test; Desachy (2004), EPPM seriograph. All three taken from the kairos reference list; section and equation numbers not checked. Packages: kairos 2.3.0 (CRAN), tabula 3.3.2 (JOSS, doi:10.21105/joss.01821). CA and transition formula: standard. Toy: cards/_toys/toys.py.

Inputs and preprocessing

  • OFR repo volumes, 2018-05 → 2026-09 (101 months). Tri-party uses OFR's TRIV1 series, which does not show the Fed reverse-repo balloon.
  • OFR split ≤30 days into 2–7 and 8–30 days on 2025-08-14; summed back into one bucket.
  • Form PF tab 8.27 total hedge-fund borrowing, 12-month log change; dated by reference month, not publication date.

What we ran

  1. CA seriation (tabula / kairos 2.3.0), EPPM plot, Porčić test with 1,000 permutations.
  2. Event model on all 101 months (R² 0.93), and an honest walk-forward version: train 2018-05 → 2022-12 (56 months), date 2023-01 → 2026-09 (45 months).
  3. Mix speed vs Form PF growth at leads 0/3/6 for total, prime-broker and reverse-repo borrowing, shift null, Holm over 18 tests; robustness without the post-split months, by sub-period and on 3-month changes.
  4. Seriation on each half separately. The full R script runs in 2.5 s.
How to read the main chart (EPPM seriograph)
  1. Rows are months in seriated order, which is close to calendar order; columns are the 9 segments, oldest-fashion on the left.
  2. Grey bars are each segment's share; black marks the part above that segment's average.
  3. Follow the black down the page: DVP >30d and tri-party overnight above average in 2018–2022, GCF term in 2023–mid-2024, DVP overnight from late 2024.
  4. A segment with black in two separate places (like GCF overnight) breaks the one-rise-one-fall rule; that is why the coefficient is 0.52, not near 1.
What it means for a decision-maker

The repo market's venue and tenor mix has been drifting steadily in one direction, toward overnight and short-term DVP, and it drifted faster after 2022. When hedge-fund borrowing grows faster, that drift speeds up three to six months later. The clock gives one number per month for "where the mix is" and a speed, which a stacked chart does not. Descriptive only.

Where it breaks (seen in our runs)

  • Changing categories: the 2025-08 bucket split moves the clock a full year even after summing back.
  • Dollars are not sherds: CA weights months by volume; tri-party is transaction volume, DVP and GCF are outstanding, so shares compare like with like only within a venue.
  • Independence: the event model's intervals assume independent deposits; months are not.
  • Linear extrapolation: trained on 2018–22, the walk-forward clock lags reality after 2023 (it reads 2023–26 as "2023–2025").
  • Type order is unstable between halves (τ 0.28): read segments from the EPPM plot, not from one axis score.

What we'd do next

Top pick (Math): treat re-defined buckets as supplementary CA columns tied by an aggregation constraint and block-bootstrap the intervals, so the clock survives the split. Then the same clock on tri-party collateral mix and on TFF open interest by trader type.

Verdict: not worth building

The rule we wrote first: build it (a Python port plus the taxonomy-robust clock) only if the clock dates unseen months on at least 3 of the 5 new open datasets and either helps on the mocks or finds a new Form PF link. It passed on 0 of 5, did not help on the mocks, and found no new link. Most of these mixes swing back and forth (collateral, positioning, borrowing type) instead of moving through one fashion cycle, which is the shape seriation needs. The one place it works, the OFR repo venue × tenor mix, is worth keeping as a Q1 chart. It isn't worth an engineering build.

1 · Engineering · Effort S
A Python port of CA seriation, the Porčić test and event dates

Three small linear-algebra routines: CA by SVD, a mode count over permutations, a regression on CA coordinates. A NumPy port, with Rust for the permutation test, runs them inside the lab's Python pipelines and on daily data with bootstrap bands. kairos 2.3.0 is the oracle.

Math sketch. \(S = D_r^{-1/2}(P - rc^\top)D_c^{-1/2} = U\Sigma V^\top\); \(F = D_r^{-1/2}U\Sigma\); modes counted on each column after ordering by \(F_{\cdot 1}\).

Test. CA coordinates ≤ 1e-10 (up to sign), event dates ≤ 1e-8, identical mode counts under shared permutations; then 10,000 permutations on 2,000 days in < 1 s.

2 · Math · Effort M · ★ Top pick
A taxonomy-robust mix clock with a time-series null

The event model treats months as independent deposits and assumes the type list never changes. Both fail here. Make re-defined buckets supplementary CA columns tied by an aggregation constraint, and bootstrap the event intervals in blocks.

Math sketch. Keep the old bucket as the active column; project the new sub-buckets \(j_1, j_2\) as supplementary columns with the constraint \(n_{i j_1} + n_{i j_2} = n_{ij}\), so post-split months get coordinates on the pre-split axes. Intervals: moving-block bootstrap of months, refit CA and \(\beta\) per draw.

Test (pre-registered). The 2025-08 jump (−0.51 → +0.50 years) shrinks below 0.15 years, and the walk-forward MAE (1.06 years) does not get worse.

Q1.4 · Tenor, venue and collateral mix

Partly answered. The OFR venue × tenor mix moves like a clock, and Form PF total borrowing growth leads its speed by 3–6 months (r = 0.55, Holm p = 0.005 on 11-17). The tri-party collateral mix does not: no clock (unseen-month error 1.7× naive) and no Form PF link (p ≥ 0.65).

Q1.5 · Positioning versus funding

No. CFTC Treasury-futures positioning by trader type has a strong first axis (80% of inertia) but no clock (1.5× naive) and no Form PF lead (p ≥ 0.86). On this tool, "where they're positioned" doesn't move with "how it's funded".

Q1.1 · Lead / lag

Only through Q1.4 above: the lead is from total borrowing, not prime-broker borrowing alone (11-15). Form PF's own creditor mix (O6) has no clock either (2.1× naive).

Q1.3 · Regime breaks

Indirect. The clock's one-year jump at OFR's 2025-08 bucket split is a data-definition break, not a market one. Treasury's TIPS relabelling and the 2014 arrival of FRNs (O5) are the same kind of trap.

Q2.4 / Q2.5 · Structure and seeing many short series (SYNTHETIC mocks)

No gain. No seriation in program × year spend (p = 0.25 and 0.27); planted-cluster recovery within ±0.03 AUC of a plain growth slope; the battleship chart is unreadable at 1,000 programs.

Still missing for Q1: a Form PF series at monthly publication timing (today's are reference-month).
