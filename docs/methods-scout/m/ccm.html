The estimator

For each time \(t\), find the \(E+1\) nearest neighbours \(t_1,\dots,t_{E+1}\) of \(\mathbf y_t\) on \(M_Y\) (from a library of \(L\) points, excluding a 4-week radius around \(t\)), with distances \(d_1\le\dots\le d_{E+1}\). Reconstruct \(x_t\) from the \(x\)-values at those times:

$$\hat x_t \mid M_Y = \sum_{i=1}^{E+1} w_i\, x_{t_i},\qquad w_i = \frac{e^{-d_i/d_1}}{\sum_{k} e^{-d_k/d_1}},\qquad \rho_L = \operatorname{cor}(x_t,\ \hat x_t).$$

"\(X\) drives \(Y\)" is read when \(\rho_L\) is positive and rises with \(L\) (convergence). Note the direction: the responder's manifold recovers the driver.

Why it works, step by step

Step 1 · delay embedding (Takens)If \(x\) and \(y\) come from one deterministic system, the lag vectors \(\mathbf y_t\) of a single observed variable trace out a copy of the whole system's attractor (a smooth one-to-one image, for \(E\) large enough). So the full state, including \(x_t\), is a function of \(\mathbf y_t\).
Step 2 · nearby on \(M_Y\) ⇒ nearby in \(x\)Because the map is smooth, points close on \(M_Y\) correspond to close system states, hence close \(x\). The weighted neighbour average \(\hat x_t\) then estimates \(x_t\).
Step 3 · convergenceWith a bigger library, neighbours are closer (\(d_i\to 0\)), so the estimate improves and \(\rho_L\) climbs. Plain correlation does not have this signature; that is what separates causal coupling from coincidence.
Step 4 · the asymmetryIf \(X\) drives \(Y\) but not the reverse, \(y\) carries information about \(x\) but \(x\)'s own dynamics carry none about \(y\): \(M_Y\) recovers \(X\), \(M_X\) does not recover \(Y\). Granger asks the opposite question ("does \(X\)'s past improve a linear forecast of \(Y\)?"), which works when variables are separable and can be fooled by shared cycles.

Inference and checks

  • Null: 200 circular shifts of the driver by ≥ 26 weeks, \(\rho\) recomputed at \(L = 350\). Keeps the driver's persistence, breaks alignment. Holm across the 10 directions; with 200 surrogates the smallest p is 0.005, and 0.005 × 10 = 0.05 after Holm.
  • Determinism pre-check: simplex and S-map skill on each series' own past; S-map with \(\theta>0\) beating \(\theta=0\) (linear) means state-dependent dynamics (spread: 0.542 vs 0.451).
  • Lag scan (Ye et al. 2015): cross-map at lags −4…+4; a peak at a negative lag fits a delayed effect (fails → spread peaks at −1 week).

Skill rises with \(L\) only in the true direction. Our real links (0.33, 0.25) converge too, but are far noisier than this toy.

How it differs from the standard tool. Granger causality fits a linear autoregression and tests whether \(X\)'s lags help predict \(Y\). It needs separable, roughly linear effects and is fooled by common drivers and shared cycles. CCM needs no model, reads state-dependent coupling, and has a built-in signature (convergence). Its own assumption is near-deterministic, weakly noisy dynamics, which our weekly differences only partly meet.

What we found

Finding 1

The spread's dynamics remember fails and positioning, not the reverse.

Cross-map skill at full library: fails → spread 0.33 (reverse 0.16); positioning → spread 0.25 (reverse 0.01). Both converge as the library grows.

Both teal links: shift p = 0.005 (the floor with 200 surrogates), Holm p = 0.05.

Finding 2

Where Granger sees a two-way link, CCM sees none.

Positioning ↔ fails: Granger p = 0.001 and 0.004; CCM p = 0.47 and 0.21. Granger-both-ways with no cross-map skill is the classic sign of a shared linear cycle (untested here).

Finding 3

Links into DVP volume appear only after mid-2022.

Fails → DVP volume: ρ 0.02 in 2019–22 vs 0.38 in 2022–25, as DVP outstanding roughly doubled. Fails → spread is stable (0.27 / 0.26).

How to read the main chart (convergence curves)
  1. x = library size \(L\); y = cross-map skill \(\rho\). Each panel is one direction (driver → responder).
  2. A link needs two things: the curve rises as \(L\) grows, and it ends above the grey band (below the null's 95th percentile).

  4. Flat curves inside the band (positioning ↔ fails) mean no detectable coupling.
What it means for a decision-maker

The DVP–tri-party rate spread behaves like the "listener": its week-to-week moves carry traces of settlement fails and of leveraged-fund positioning, more than the reverse. Fails and positioning are not linked to each other, even though a standard linear test says they are; that test is probably picking up shared calendar effects. These links are real but modest and sit right at the edge of significance. Descriptive only.

Where it breaks (seen in our runs)

  • Noise: CCM assumes near-deterministic dynamics; ours are noisy weekly differences with \(\rho\) of 0.2–0.3.
  • p-value floor: 200 surrogates cap p at 0.005; after Holm the two links sit exactly at 0.05.
  • Unpredictable driver: positioning changes are nearly unpredictable from their own past (\(\rho\approx0.07\)), so they can only be tested as a driver.
  • Regime change: links into DVP volume exist only after mid-2022, so full-sample numbers mix two regimes.
  • Circular shift wraps the end of each series onto its start.

1 · Engineering · Effort S–M · ★ Top pick
Surrogate nulls for free: reuse the neighbour sets

In a shift-surrogate test the responder's shadow manifold never changes; only the driver values being predicted move. Find neighbours once; each surrogate then costs \(O(nk)\). A Rust core with this reuse, checked against pyEDM, makes 10,000 surrogates cheap and lifts today's p-value floor (1/201).

Math sketch. Neighbour indices \(t_i(t)\) and weights \(w_i(t)\) depend only on \(M_Y\). For a shifted driver \(x^{(\tau)}_t = x_{(t+\tau)\bmod n}\): \(\hat x^{(\tau)}_t = \sum_i w_i(t)\,x^{(\tau)}_{t_i(t)}\), a sparse matrix–vector product per surrogate.

Test. ρ parity ≤ 1e-12 with pyEDM 2.5.7 on all 10 directions at full library; the 10 × 200 null in < 1 s (today: 148 s).

2 · Math · Effort M
A familywise partial-CCM network

Condition each cross map on the third and fourth legs (partial cross mapping) to separate direct from indirect links, with persistence-preserving surrogates and max-T control over every edge of the 4-node graph.

Math sketch. Compare \(\rho(x, \hat x\mid M_Y)\) with the skill of an indirect route \(\rho(x, \widehat{x}\mid \widehat{M_Z}\text{ from } M_Y)\); an edge survives if the direct skill exceeds the best indirect route, with max-T over the 12 directed edges.

Test. On simulated 4-node chains at our persistence: size 5% ± 1.5 points, indirect edges removed ≥ 80% of the time. Then: does positioning → spread survive conditioning on fails?

3 · New use on our data · Effort S
Rolling CCM on the late-2025 switch-off (Q1.2–Q1.3)

CRQA showed the fails–spread coupling turning off after July 2025. Rolling 2-year CCM would show whether the direction (fails → spread) faded first, or positioning → spread took over.

Math sketch. \(\rho_w\) at fixed \(L\) in each 2-year window \(w\), a surrogate null per window, Holm across windows.

Test. Windows and episodes fixed in advance; an honest miss counts.
