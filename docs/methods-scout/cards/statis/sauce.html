One multi-table engine for unbalanced panels

A NumPy core (Rust if Q2 scale needs it) for STATIS, COVSTATIS, partial triadic analysis and DISTATIS that accepts firms or programs entering and leaving, with exact era permutations and block bootstraps built in. ade4 is the oracle (parity already at 1e-13 on balanced windows); DistatisR is the second oracle once installable.

Math sketch. For unbalanced object sets, compute each table's cross-product on its own objects, then embed it in the union with zero rows/columns and a per-table mask \(M_k\); use \(\mathrm{RV}_M(S_a,S_b)=\langle M_{ab}\circ S_a,M_{ab}\circ S_b\rangle/\|M_{ab}\circ S_a\|\|M_{ab}\circ S_b\|\) on the shared objects \(M_{ab}=M_a\wedge M_b\).

Why it matters here. Only 11 firms are in all 13 exercises; Q2 programs start and stop. Today we had to switch to variable-by-variable tables to avoid dropping firms.

Closest prior work: ade4 statis/pta (balanced only; docs read); DistatisR (balanced cubes).

Test (pass bar set now). Parity ≤ 1e-12 with ade4 on balanced cases; on balanced data with the mask path, identical results; the Q2 Morgan Stanley mock (1,840 programs × 5 years × features) in under 1 s.

2 · Math · Effort M · ★ Top pick
A reliability-corrected RV for small samples

Our year-level check failed because RV computed from 18–35 firms is pulled around by sampling noise. Correct each year's RV for the reliability of its own correlation matrix, the way psychometrics corrects a correlation for attenuation.

Math sketch. Split each year's firms at random into halves \(h,h'\); reliability \(\hat\rho_k=\overline{\mathrm{RV}(S_k^{h},S_k^{h'})}\) over many splits, stepped up (Spearman–Brown) \(\rho_k=2\hat\rho_k/(1+\hat\rho_k)\). Corrected \(\mathrm{RV}^{c}_{ab}=\mathrm{RV}_{ab}/\sqrt{\rho_a\rho_b}\), capped at 1, on the modified (diagonal-free) RV of Smilde et al. Bootstrap the whole procedure for intervals.

Why it matters here. It would let us say which single exercises (2019? 2022?) depart, not just that two eras differ, and compare years with 18 firms to years with 35.

Closest prior work: Smilde et al. (2009, Bioinformatics 25(3):401–405), modified RV; Josse, Pagès & Husson (2008, CSDA 53(1):82–91), RV tests. No split-half disattenuated RV found.

Test (pass bar set now). Simulation at our design (13 tables, 18–35 objects, 7 variables, known true structures with a planted break): bias of the corrected RV within ±0.01 of the truth (raw RV and modified RV as comparators), 95% bootstrap coverage between 93% and 97%, and the planted break year detected in ≥ 80% of runs at a 5% false-flag rate.

3 · New use on our data · Effort S
Partial triadic paths on the Q2 mock budgets (Q2.2, Q2.4)

Treat each fiscal year as a table (programs × spend, headcount, text-implied features). Programs whose path through the compromise map is longest are candidates for "quietly changed category" (Q2.2); groups whose paths run parallel are hidden co-movers (Q2.4).

Math sketch. Partial points \(F_{k,i}\) for program \(i\) in year \(k\); path length \(L_i=\sum_k\|F_{k+1,i}-F_{k,i}\|\); flag the top \(L_i\) within each segment. For Q2.4, cluster programs on their partial trajectories \((F_{1,i},\dots,F_{K,i})\).

Why it matters here. It uses all features at once, which neither ebnm (one number per program) nor DenseLines (one series per program) does.

Closest prior work: ade4 partial triadic analysis (Thioulouse 2011 comparison, title found, not read). No budget application found.

Test (pass bar set now). SYNTHETIC mocks only: AUC of \(L_i\) against the planted category-switch key ≥ 0.75 and above a baseline of raw year-to-year feature change; a new-seed mock for confirmation.
