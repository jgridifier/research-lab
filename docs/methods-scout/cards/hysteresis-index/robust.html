Established, light on inference

What the home field has established

  • Origin and the index we used. Lloyd, Freer, Johnes & Collins (2016, HESS 20:625–632; read) review ratio-based hysteresis indices (Lawler et al. 2006), show they depend on the storm's starting level and size (Fig. 1), and propose the difference index on rescaled data. On 66 River Wylye storms (15-minute turbidity and discharge), 5% discharge steps gave stable index distributions for 98% of storms (Table 1). That storm data is not published with the paper, so we reproduced only the Fig. 1 values (exactly).
  • Adoption. Vaughan et al. (2017, WRR; §3.4 read in a search extract) use the Lloyd index with a flushing index for nitrate and DOC storms; a 2024 WRR review of suspended-sediment hysteresis (doi:10.1029/2024wr037216; search extract read) calls the Lloyd HI and Vaughan FI "widely adopted" and recommends the differential index for complex and figure-eight loops. R package loadflux carries other indices (SHI, Aich's AHI); a 2026 reproducibility repository (Tabo, Jacobs & Breuer) implements Lloyd HI, loop area and FI in R (code listing read; not run).
  • Known failure modes. An exactly symmetric figure-of-eight gives 0 (Lloyd §2.3); results depend on how events are cut and on the rescaling; the index has no standard significance test, so storms are usually compared descriptively (Kruskal–Wallis across sites in Vaughan et al.).

Verdict: established, light on inference. The index is simple, well specified, and in routine use across catchment studies, and its published worked values reproduce. But its validation is about stability and comparability, not about error rates, and every user picks their own storm rule. Here the null had to be built (circular shifts), and a calendar confound showed up that the home field would not face.
