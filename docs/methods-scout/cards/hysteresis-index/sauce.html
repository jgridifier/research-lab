A storm-loop module for any pair of series

One small module: storm finder (pre-registered rule as parameters), Lloyd HI, flushing index, loop area, circular-shift and block nulls, weekly or daily, with the loop-grid chart. Today's code in runs/2026-11-18/hyst/ is the start.

Math sketch. For each storm precompute the limb crossing points of \(\hat Q\) once; a shift of \(C\) only changes which \(C\) values are read, so each surrogate is a gather of \(C\) at fixed (index, weight) pairs: \(C^{\uparrow}(q_j)=(1-w_j)C_{i_j}+w_jC_{i_j+1}\), then rescale per storm.

Why it matters here. Q1 has a dozen candidate series (venues, tenors, fail types, positioning by contract) and two frequencies; loops are the most readable view of "who moves first in stress".

Closest prior work: loadflux (R; SHI, AHI) and the Mau reproducibility R script (Lloyd HI, loop area, FI); neither has a null.

Test (pass bar set now). Lloyd Fig. 1 values exact; sine loops ±0.575 at ∓0.6 rad; 13 storms × 4,999 shifts in under 2 s on the box (today's loop: about 19 s).

A calendar-locked null (Q1.2)

Shift the response series only by whole quarters (13 weeks), so quarter-end weeks in \(C\) always land on quarter-end weeks in time, then ask whether the loop is still more anticlockwise than chance. This separates "spread lags fails" from "storms end at quarter-ends".

Math sketch. Let \(S=\{13k:\ k=1,\dots,\lfloor L/13\rfloor-1\}\). Under \(H_0\): \(C\) and \(Q\) are unrelated beyond a shared quarterly calendar, so \(\overline{\mathrm{HI}}(C_{\cdot+s})\), \(s\in S\), are exchangeable with the observed one. With \(L=436\) weeks there are 32 shifts, so the smallest attainable p is \(1/33\approx 0.03\) (computed); add within-quarter block permutation of non-quarter-end weeks to get more resolution.

Why it matters here. Today's loop drops from −0.17 to −0.08 when quarter-end weeks are masked; masking throws data away, a locked null does not.

Closest prior work: none found in the hysteresis literature; calendar-preserving surrogates are standard in climate (seasonal block bootstrap) but not used with this index.

Test (pass bar set now). The spread loop counts as real only if the mean HI is below the 5th percentile of the calendar-locked null (one-sided, direction fixed by today's sign) and stays negative with the 44 quarter-end weeks masked.

3 · New use on our data · Effort S
Loops around leveraged-fund unwinds (Q1.5)

The 11-17 ECA found fails spikes one week after big leveraged-fund unwinds. Use the unwind weeks as "storms" (discharge = size of the unwind, cumulated) and draw fails and the spread as responses: does fails come after the unwind inside every episode, or only in March 2020?

Math sketch. Storm on \(Q_t=-\sum_{u\le t}\Delta\mathrm{NS}_u\) over a fixed 8-week window around each bottom-10% unwind week; HI of fails on that loop; a clockwise loop (positive HI) means fails rise after positioning has moved.

Why it matters here. It turns a count ("26% of spikes follow an unwind") into an episode-by-episode picture that shows which episodes carry the link.

Closest prior work: 11-17 ECA card (#70) in this lab.

Test (pass bar set now). Fails loops have HI > 0 in at least two thirds of unwind episodes and the mean HI beats the circular-shift null at p < 0.05; repeated with March 2020 removed.
