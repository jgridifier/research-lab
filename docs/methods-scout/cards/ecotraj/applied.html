Inputs and preprocessing

  • 11-12 scout (PROXY): FR Y-9C trading assets of 14 dealer holding companies, 2016Q3–2026Q2; equity trading revenue of JPM, GS, MS, BAC (E4).
  • 11-17 new use (live Q1 data): OFR repo, monthly 2018-05 → 2026-09: venue × tenor volumes (9 segments, 101 months); tri-party collateral volumes (Treasury, agency, corporate debt, other), 2014-08 → 2026-09 (146 months). OFR's 2025-08 tenor split is summed back. Market: monthly mean VIX, monthly mean 10-year Treasury yield, log total panel volume.
  • Simulations: 14 parts (6 in P2), 4 market covariates, T = 40 and 62, planted sticky swap of 0.10 per step (smaller in P2).

What we ran

  1. 11-12: ecotraj 1.2.2 on the proxy panels; the increment split found a directional residual walk the level split hid.
  2. 11-14 Extension 1 (Engineering): clean-room Python port (jgridifier/ecotraj-py) with randomized R-oracle parity and numba kernels.
  3. 11-14 Extension 2 (Math): pre-registered simulation of size and power; merged before running.
  4. 11-17 Extension 3 (New use): the Extension 2 procedure, unchanged, on the two OFR panels; 999 sign-flip draws; Holm over 2. Plus a DSPD map of stress episodes.
  5. 11-17 P2: pre-registered power curve for smaller swaps and fewer parts. The size check passes (6 parts: 0.051 at T = 40, 0.042 at T = 62). Minimum detectable swap at T = 62: 0.075 per step (14 parts) and 0.10 (6 parts). At T = 40, 0.10 gives 92% (14 parts) and 62% (6 parts). The Wilcoxon and level baselines stay well below P1 in every cell.
How to read the main chart (directionality)
  1. Each bar is how consistently a residual path keeps its heading: 1 = straight line, about 0.5 = turns at random.
  2. The p-value compares it with paths built from the same steps with random signs.
  3. Tri-party collateral sits at 0.628 (p = 0.036): its non-market drift, corporate debt up and Treasury down, is more one-way than chance, but not after correcting for testing two panels (Holm 0.072).
  4. Venue × tenor (0.560, p = 0.164) shows no non-market direction: after the market, its moves look like wandering.
What it means for a decision-maker

The tool now does what it claims: on simulated data it finds a steady non-market shift almost every time and raises a false alarm only about 5% of the time. On the real repo data, the collateral mix shows a hint of a steady move from Treasuries toward corporate debt beyond what volatility, rates and volume explain, but the evidence isn't strong enough to call it. Where it breaks (seen in our runs)

  • Market measured badly → wrong split. The split is only as good as \(X_t\); three monthly covariates are a coarse market.
  • Data-definition changes (OFR's 2025-08 tenor split) create steps that are not behaviour.
  • Parity: 20 of 1,280 randomized checks differ from R on exactly collinear points (R floating-point noise); none touches directionality, the null or the split.
  • Stationary levels (no drift, mean-reverting mix): the test is conservative (0.0% false alarms), so it can under-call.
