Assumptions

  • All shares strictly positive (clr exists).
  • Market effects enter the clr increments linearly through observed \(\Delta X_t\).
  • Under the null, residual increments are symmetric around zero and roughly exchangeable in sign (what the sign-flip uses).

Why it works, step by step

Step 1 · clr makes geometry validEuclidean distance on clr coordinates equals the Aitchison distance on the simplex, so lengths and angles of the path are statistics of the mixes themselves, unchanged by reordering parts.
Step 2 · the split adds back exactlyEach row of \(\Delta C\) sums to zero, so each row of the fitted \(\Delta X\hat B\) and of \(\hat E\) also sums to zero. Therefore \(C_t = C_1 + m_t + r_t\) exactly, and in shares \(S_t = S_1 \oplus \operatorname{softmax}(m_t) \oplus \operatorname{softmax}(r_t)\): the market and residual sub-paths are themselves valid mixes.
Step 3 · why levels hide a driftRegressing clr levels on \([1,X_t]\) lets a persistent market covariate, which is itself a near-random walk, soak up a slow residual drift. Differencing first removes that: \(\Delta X_t\) is close to independent noise, so it cannot track a steady drift. Simulation: level split finds 1.3% of planted swaps, increment split 99.7%.
Step 4 · the nullFlip the sign of each residual step at random, re-project with \(M\) so the null path lives in the same space as the observed residuals, rebuild the path and recompute dir. 199 (simulations) or 999 (data) draws; \(p = (1+\#\{\text{null}\ge\text{obs}\})/(1+B)\).

Multiple testing: Holm across panels (two on 11-17). Size is checked by simulation, not assumed: re-projection makes it approximate (Freedman–Lane / ter Braak style).

Toy example (computed on the box)

Path \(y_1=(0,0)\), \(y_2=(1,0)\), \(y_3=(2,1)\). One triple: \(s_{12}=1\), \(s_{23}=1.414\), angle between the legs 45°, so dir = 1 − 45/180 = 0.75.

Sign-flip null with 2 steps: flipping both or neither keeps the 45° turn (dir 0.75); flipping one makes it a 135° turn (dir 0.25). Two of the four sign vectors reach 0.75, so p = 2/4 = 0.5. Two steps can never be significant; the real panels have 61–145.

How it differs from the standard tool. The usual view is a stacked-area chart of shares, or a regression of each share on market variables. Neither asks whether the path keeps one direction, and share regressions ignore the simplex. ETA works on the whole mix at once with valid geometry, and the increment split separates "everyone moved with the market" from "this mix drifted on its own".
