The estimator

Score a predicted CDF \(F\) against an outcome \(y\) by

$$\mathrm{CRPS}(F,y) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big(F(z) - \mathbf 1[y\le z]\big)^2\,dz .$$

IDR picks the conditional CDFs that minimise total CRPS over the training data, subject only to stochastic order:

$$\begin{gathered}\min_{F_{x_1},\dots,F_{x_n}} \sum_{i=1}^n \mathrm{CRPS}(F_{x_i}, y_i)\\ \text{s.t. } x_i \preceq x_j \Rightarrow F_{x_i}(z) \ge F_{x_j}(z)\ \ \forall z .\end{gathered}$$

A more favourable covariate never predicts a stochastically smaller payout. No bandwidth, link, or distribution family; only the direction.

Why it works, step by step

Step 1 · CRPS splits into thresholdsThe integrand at each \(z\) only involves \(F(z)\) and \(\mathbf 1[y\le z]\). So minimising the integral means minimising, separately at every threshold \(z\), \(\sum_i (\eta_i - \mathbf 1[y_i \le z])^2\) over \(\eta_i = F_{x_i}(z)\).
Step 2 · each threshold is an isotonic regressionThe order constraint becomes "\(\eta\) is non-increasing along the order", so each threshold is a least-squares isotonic fit of a 0/1 sequence, solved exactly by pool-adjacent-violators (PAVA) in one dimension (paper §2.3, eq. 15). The objective only changes at observed \(y\) values, so a finite number of thresholds suffices.
Step 3 · the pieces fit togetherPAVA on nested indicator sequences gives values that are also monotone in \(z\), so the threshold fits stack into valid CDFs. The paper shows the solution is the same for a wide class of proper scores, not just CRPS ("universality", §2.2), and that it is calibrated.
Step 4 · skill and the nullSkill \(= 1 - \overline{\mathrm{CRPS}}_{\text{IDR}}/\overline{\mathrm{CRPS}}_{\text{clim}}\), where climatology predicts the training sample's empirical distribution for everyone. Evaluation leaves one test year out. Null: shuffle the covariate within each year 299 times and refit (smallest possible p = 1/300 ≈ 0.003).
Toy example (computed on the box)

Four firms ordered by pre-tax income, \(x = 1,2,3,4\), with payouts \(y = 0.5, 2.0, 1.0, 3.0\). At threshold \(z = 1.0\) the indicators \(\mathbf 1[y\le 1]\) are \((1, 0, 1, 0)\). The CDF must be non-increasing in \(x\), but \(0\) then \(1\) violates it, so PAVA pools firms 2 and 3: \(\hat F(1.0) = (1, 0.5, 0.5, 0)\). At \(z = 0.5\): \((1,0,0,0)\); at \(z = 2.0\): \((1,1,1,0)\). Firm 3's predictive distribution is therefore 50% at or below 1.0 and 100% at or below 2.0: a step CDF learned with no tuning.

How it differs from the standard tool. OLS with normal errors predicts a mean and a symmetric bell. Payouts are skewed and some firms issue net shares (down to −1.4% of RWA), so on the raw scale OLS scored worse than climatology (−2.2%) while IDR gained 5.8%. Quantile regression gives a few quantiles under a linear form; IDR gives every quantile under only a monotone form. The price: step functions that are coarse at our n, and a weak partial order with two or more covariates.
