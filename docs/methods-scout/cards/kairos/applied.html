Inputs and preprocessing

  • OFR repo volumes, 2018-05 → 2026-09 (101 months). Tri-party uses OFR's TRIV1 series, which does not show the Fed reverse-repo balloon.
  • OFR split ≤30 days into 2–7 and 8–30 days on 2025-08-14; summed back into one bucket.
  • Form PF tab 8.27 total hedge-fund borrowing, 12-month log change; dated by reference month, not publication date.

What we ran

  1. CA seriation (tabula / kairos 2.3.0), EPPM plot, Porčić test with 1,000 permutations.
  2. Event model on all 101 months (R² 0.93), and an honest walk-forward version: train 2018-05 → 2022-12 (56 months), date 2023-01 → 2026-09 (45 months).
  3. Mix speed vs Form PF growth at leads 0/3/6 for total, prime-broker and reverse-repo borrowing, shift null, Holm over 18 tests; robustness without the post-split months, by sub-period and on 3-month changes.
  4. Seriation on each half separately. The full R script runs in 2.5 s.
How to read the main chart (EPPM seriograph)
  1. Rows are months in seriated order, which is close to calendar order; columns are the 9 segments, oldest-fashion on the left.
  2. Grey bars are each segment's share; black marks the part above that segment's average.
  3. Follow the black down the page: DVP >30d and tri-party overnight above average in 2018–2022, GCF term in 2023–mid-2024, DVP overnight from late 2024.
  4. A segment with black in two separate places (like GCF overnight) breaks the one-rise-one-fall rule; that is why the coefficient is 0.52, not near 1.
What it means for a decision-maker

The repo market's venue and tenor mix has been drifting steadily in one direction, toward overnight and short-term DVP, and it drifted faster after 2022. When hedge-fund borrowing grows faster, that drift speeds up three to six months later. The clock gives one number per month for "where the mix is" and a speed, which a stacked chart does not. Descriptive only.

Where it breaks (seen in our runs)

  • Changing categories: the 2025-08 bucket split moves the clock a full year even after summing back.
  • Dollars are not sherds: CA weights months by volume; tri-party is transaction volume, DVP and GCF are outstanding, so shares compare like with like only within a venue.
  • Independence: the event model's intervals assume independent deposits; months are not.
  • Linear extrapolation: trained on 2018–22, the walk-forward clock lags reality after 2023 (it reads 2023–26 as "2023–2025").
  • Type order is unstable between halves (τ 0.28): read segments from the EPPM plot, not from one axis score.

What we'd do next

Top pick (Math): treat re-defined buckets as supplementary CA columns tied by an aggregation constraint and block-bootstrap the intervals, so the clock survives the split. Then the same clock on tri-party collateral mix and on TFF open interest by trader type.

Verdict: not worth building

The rule we wrote first: build it (a Python port plus the taxonomy-robust clock) only if the clock dates unseen months on at least 3 of the 5 new open datasets and either helps on the mocks or finds a new Form PF link. It passed on 0 of 5, did not help on the mocks, and found no new link. Most of these mixes swing back and forth (collateral, positioning, borrowing type) instead of moving through one fashion cycle, which is the shape seriation needs. The one place it works, the OFR repo venue × tenor mix, is worth keeping as a Q1 chart. It isn't worth an engineering build.
