1 · The problem ECA solves, and the ones it does not

ECA takes two binary event series on the same clock, \(A\) (say, fails-spike weeks) and \(B\) (big leveraged-fund unwind weeks), a lag \(\tau\) and a tolerance window \(\Delta T\). It reports two directed rates and tests each against a null (Donges et al. 2016; Siegmund, Siegmund & Donner 2017):

$$r_p=\Pr\big(\text{a }B\text{ event in }[t-\tau-\Delta T,\,t-\tau]\ \big|\ A\text{ event at }t\big),\qquad r_t=\Pr\big(\text{an }A\text{ event in }[s+\tau,\,s+\tau+\Delta T]\ \big|\ B\text{ event at }s\big)$$

Read as plain probabilities, the precursor rate is a hit rate ("what share of fails spikes had an unwind just before?") and the trigger rate is a precision ("what share of unwinds were followed by a fails spike?"). These two readings are ours; the definitions are the paper's.

Good fit: ECA answers itBad fit: use something else
Do extreme \(B\) weeks come before extreme \(A\) weeks more often than chance, at a stated lag and window?How big is the effect? ("each $10bn unwind adds X to fails.") ECA throws away magnitudes. Use a regression or a Hawkes model with marks.
Is the link one-directional? (\(B\to A\) vs \(A\to B\)) and one-tailed? (unwinds vs builds)Dependence in ordinary weeks (the body of the distribution). Use correlation, Granger or transfer entropy.
Small event counts: tens of events, where fitting a model would overfit (we had 39 fails-spike weeks)."What would have happened without the unwind?" That is an intervention question. Needs an assignment mechanism (section 2).
Is the link stable over time? (rolling or split-sample rates)Mechanism or path. ECA sees two event series, not the plumbing between them.
Does the link survive once a third event series is held out or required? (conditional ECA)Many continuous confounders at once. Use PCMCI-style conditional-independence discovery.
Screening many pairs cheaply before deciding where to model.Problems where the event definition is the open question: results depend on the threshold, which must be fixed in advance.

2 · Where the line on causality is

It helps to put the methods on a ladder. Each rung needs more assumptions and gives a stronger claim.

Rung 1 · associationTwo series move together. Cross-correlation lives here (r = −0.16 at lag 1 on our data).
Rung 2 · association with time order · ECA lives hereExtreme \(B\) weeks come before extreme \(A\) weeks more often than a null that keeps each series' own timing. The originators say so plainly: for their flood → epidemic result, "no direct causal attribution is possible based on this test" (Donges et al. 2016, arXiv v2, results section). Siegmund et al. (2016) frame "\(B\) causally influences the timing of \(A\)" as the hypothesis to be tested, not the conclusion. A significant rate rules out "chance alignment given the null"; it does not rule out a common driver, and it says nothing about what an intervention would do.
Rung 3 · predictive (Granger-type) causalityPast \(B\) improves the prediction of \(A\) beyond past \(A\) and everything else in the information set (Granger 1969). Transfer entropy is the model-free version (Schreiber 2000); point-process Granger does it for event trains (Kim et al. 2011). Conditional ECA is a coarse event-level step towards this rung: it conditions on one or two other event series, not on their full values. Thresholding a confounder leaves its sub-threshold variation unconditioned, so some confounding can remain (our reasoning, not a cited result).
Rung 4 · causal discovery from observational dataPCMCI reads a lagged causal graph from conditional independences, under causal sufficiency (no unobserved common causes), the causal Markov condition and faithfulness (Runge 2018; Runge et al. 2019). Convergent cross mapping (Sugihara et al. 2012) targets weakly coupled deterministic dynamics instead. Both need long, mostly continuous series.
Rung 5 · interventional causalityEffects defined as differences in potential outcomes, which need a known or as-good-as-random assignment mechanism (Bojinov & Shephard 2019 for single time series). The finance event study (MacKinlay 1997) is causal to the extent that the event date is unrelated to other news; that is a design assumption, not something the test checks.
The line, in one sentence. ECA can tell you "fails spikes follow unwinds one week later far more often than chance, and not the other way round"; it cannot tell you "unwinds cause fails". With conditional ECA it can add "and this is not just the weeks where VIX also spiked". Going further needs either many more observations (rungs 3–4) or a source of as-good-as-random variation (rung 5). On our 11-15/11-17 data the ladder already showed up: linear Granger rejected in both directions, CCM found nothing, and ECA located a one-way link in the unwind tail only.

4 · What gives ECA its edge

Edge 1 · Few events, tails only

It needs no model for the body of the distribution, so 30–40 events can be enough. On our data the bulk cross-correlation was weak (r = −0.16), while ECA showed 26% of fails spikes one week after a big unwind vs about 10% by chance (circular-shift p = 0.008).

Edge 2 · Sign and direction kept apart

Unwinds and builds are tested separately, and \(B\to A\) separately from \(A\to B\). Linear tools add a big build and a big unwind together with opposite signs. On our data builds showed nothing (10%, p = 0.54) and the reverse direction was not significant (15%, p = 0.16).

Edge 3 · Decision-ready rates

The two rates read directly as the hit rate and the precision of "\(B\) happened" as a warning flag for \(A\), each with its own null. No model translation is needed for a decision-maker.

Edge 4 · An explicit, swappable null

The null is a stated model of "no link": Poisson, shuffles, or surrogates that keep each series' clustering (Donges et al. 2016; CoinCalc). You can see what you assumed and change it. ECA is also less fooled by event clustering than event synchronization (Odenweller & Donner 2020).

Edge 5 · Cheap

With prefix sums each test is linear in the series length, and a circular shift is an index offset (Engineering card). So a full lag × window × threshold grid with thousands of surrogates is affordable. That matters because the honest answer to "which lag?" needs the whole grid (extension 3).

What it gives up. Magnitudes; the choice of threshold and window (must be pre-registered); and confounders beyond one or two event series. Those limits are why the extensions below exist.

5 · Extensions that add depth but keep it lean

Ranked by what they unlock per unit of effort. All are proposals except where a source already does them.

E1 · Partial and conditional ECA · Effort S · ★ first
Hold the common shock out, or require it

What exists. Siegmund et al. (2016) introduced conditional ECA (CECA, "\(B\) coincides with \(A\) only when \(C\) is also present") and joint ECA (JECA). CoinCalc 2.0 ships CC.ceca.ts.

Proposal: partial ECA ("does the link hold when \(C\) is absent?"). Keep only \(A\) events with no \(C\) event in their window, \(\mathcal A_{\neg C}=\{i:\ C\cap[t_i-\tau-\Delta T,\,t_i]=\varnothing\}\):

$$r_p(A\mid B,\neg C)=\frac1{|\mathcal A_{\neg C}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal A_{\neg C}}\Theta\Big[\textstyle\sum_j\mathbf 1_{[0,\Delta T]}\big(t_i-\tau-t^B_j\big)\Big]$$
Step 1 · what the null must keepThe hypothesis is "\(B\) tells us nothing about \(A\) once \(C\) is accounted for". Shifting \(B\) circularly keeps \(A\), \(C\) and \(B\)'s own clustering, but also breaks the \(B\)–\(C\) link.
Step 2 · which way that errsIf \(B\) events cluster near \(C\) events (unwinds near VIX spikes), then in the real data \(B\) is rarer than average near \(C\)-free \(A\) events. The shifted \(B\) is spread evenly, so it lands near \(C\)-free \(A\) events at the average rate. The null rate is therefore too high and the test is conservative. If \(B\) and \(C\) avoided each other, it would be anti-conservative. So the sign of the \(B\)–\(C\) association has to be measured and reported next to the result (our derivation).
Step 3 · a sharper null (proposal)Shift \(B\) only by amounts that keep its overlap with \(C\) within a tolerance. This is an event-series version of the local permutation scheme Runge (2018, AISTATS) uses for conditional-independence tests, which permutes \(X\) only among neighbours in \(Z\).
Toy (computed by cards/_toys/toys.py)

Same ten weeks as the math tab (\(A\) = {3, 7}, \(B\) = {2, 6, 9}, \(\Delta T=1\), \(\tau=0\)), plus a \(C\) event in week 3. The \(A\) event in week 3 has \(C\) in its window, so it is dropped. Week 7 remains, and it has \(B\) in week 6: \(r_p(A\mid B,\neg C)=1/1=1.0\). With one event left nothing can be significant. This is the power cost of conditioning, and why section 6 checks power first.

What it unlocks. The core of Q1.2/Q1.5: telling "unwinds lead to fails" apart from "a volatility shock drives both". This is the step that moves ECA as far up the causality ladder as event data allows.

E2 · Nulls that respect clustering and seasonality · Effort S–M

What exists. Surrogate testing in general (Theiler et al. 1992; Schreiber & Schmitz 2000); ECA tests for point processes with a prescribed inter-event-time distribution (Donges et al. 2016); circular shifts (our 11-17 run); the moving-block bootstrap for dependent data (Künsch 1989).

Proposals. (a) Year-shift surrogates: circular shifts by whole years only, which keep any calendar pattern such as quarter-end effects. Whether fails have one is still to be checked. (b) The two-state Markov analytic null from the Math card, which gives calibrated p-values without surrogates.

What it unlocks. p-values you can trust on clustered weekly funding data. The textbook Poisson test rejected 9.2–9.4% of independent-by-construction pairs at nominal 5%.

E3 · Honest grids: max-T over (τ, ΔT, threshold) · Effort S

What exists. Holm (1979), which we used over 48 tests; resampling-based max-T and step-down adjustment (Westfall & Young 1993), which uses the joint null of all tests.

Proposal. Compute every grid cell on the same circular shift of \(B\), take the maximum standardised rate per shift, and compare each observed cell with that maximum's distribution. Neighbouring lags are strongly correlated, so this should lose less power than Holm, which treats them as unrelated. That is a hypothesis, to be shown in simulation.

What it unlocks. Scanning the whole lag × window × threshold grid and still making a familywise-controlled claim about which lag is best, instead of picking one in advance.

E4 · The Hawkes bridge: from a rate to an intensity · Effort M

Setup (proposal). Let \(A\) have intensity \(\lambda_A(t)=\mu_A+\sum_j\varphi(t-t^B_j)\) given \(B\) (\(B\) excites \(A\), no self-excitation; Hawkes 1971). Given \(B\), \(A\) is then a Poisson process with that intensity.

Step 1For the window \(W_j=[s_j+\tau,\,s_j+\tau+\Delta T]\) after \(B\) event \(j\), \(\Pr(\text{no }A\text{ in }W_j\mid B)=\exp\!\big(-\!\int_{W_j}\lambda_A\big)\).
Step 2If no other \(B\) event's kernel reaches into \(W_j\) (sparse \(B\)), \(\int_{W_j}\lambda_A=\mu_A|W|+\Phi\) with \(\Phi=\int_\tau^{\tau+\Delta T}\varphi(u)\,du\), the kernel mass in the window.
Step 3So \(\mathbb E[r_t]=1-e^{-\mu_A|W|-\Phi}\). Inverting gives a plug-in estimate $$\hat\Phi=-\ln(1-\hat r_t)-\hat\mu_A|W|.$$ The null \(\Phi=0\) gives back the chance level \(1-e^{-\mu_A|W|}\).
Toy (computed by cards/_toys/toys.py)

Background rate \(\mu_A=0.10\) per week and \(|W|=2\) weeks: the chance trigger rate is \(1-e^{-0.2}=0.181\). An observed \(r_t=0.40\) implies \(\hat\Phi=-\ln 0.6-0.2=0.311\). The window's expected \(A\) count is then \((0.2+0.311)/0.2=2.55\) times background. Check: \(1-e^{-0.511}=0.40\).

Caveat. Fails spikes self-cluster (9% repeat the next week), and that breaks step 1. A full bivariate Hawkes fit with self-excitation (Embrechts et al. 2011) is the honest version, and it is only feasible if there are enough events. In a Hawkes model, "Granger-causes" means exactly that the cross kernel is nonzero (Eichler, Dahlhaus & Dueck 2017), which ties ECA to rung 3.

What it unlocks. "How much more likely" as an intensity multiple that can be compared across thresholds and series, and a path from a screening statistic to a generative model for stress scenarios.

E5 · Soft windows and an optimal-transport view · Effort M · low priority

What exists. Spike-train metrics that price shifting an event by \(q|\Delta t|\) against deleting it (Victor & Purpura 1996), and an Earth Mover's (Wasserstein) spike-train distance (Sihn & Kim 2019).

Proposal. Replace ECA's hard 0/1 window with a kernel weight, or with a partial optimal-transport matching between \(B\) and \(A\) events at cost \(|t^A-t^B-\tau|\) and a fixed cost for leaving an event unmatched. The transport plan's displacements give a whole lag distribution instead of a yes/no at one \(\tau\).

Honest assessment. The OT link is real: ECA's window is a hard-threshold matching. But it mainly helps with "what is the lag distribution?", and with about 40 events a histogram of matched lags carries most of that information. Matching is also one-to-one, while ECA lets several \(A\) events share one \(B\). Worth doing only if E1–E3 show a link worth characterising.

E6 · Many series at once · Effort S given E3

What exists. The event-series module in pyunicorn (PIK), which carries ECA, and JECA for joint triggers (Siegmund et al. 2016).

Proposal. A precursor / trigger network across the dozen Q1 weekly series (venues, tenors, fail types, positioning by contract).

What it unlocks. A map of which stress events lead which. It needs E3, or the number of tests (several thousand) swamps it.

6 · Proposed pre-registered next step (not run)

Proposal · to be merged as a PREREG before any run

Question. Does "fails spike one week after a big leveraged-fund unwind" survive once weeks with a volatility shock or a repo-spread shock are held out?

Step 0: power and size on SYNTHETIC data, before touching real data. Simulate two-state Markov event series with our clustering (fails repeat 9%, positioning 18%), about 40 events in \(T=399\) weeks, and an optional planted coupling that gives \(r_p\approx0.26\) against a chance level near 0.10 (the observed sizes). Also simulate a confounder \(C\) that drives both series with no \(B\to A\) link. Gate: size between 3% and 7% at nominal 5%, power ≥ 0.6 with a real link, and false rejection ≤ 7% in the confounder-only world. If the gate fails, stop: report "underpowered" and do not run the real test.

Step 1: the test. \(A\) = financing-fails spike (top 10% weekly log change), \(B\) = big unwind (bottom 10% change in leveraged-fund net short, UST 2Y+5Y+10Y), \(\tau=1\), \(\Delta T=0\). Two conditioners, each tested separately: \(C_1\) = VIX top-10% weekly change (FRED VIXCLS), \(C_2\) = DVP-spread top-10% change. Statistic: partial precursor rate \(r_p(A\mid B,\neg C_k)\). Null: 4,999 circular shifts of \(B\) (shift ≥ 8 weeks) with \(A\) and \(C_k\) fixed. Report the measured \(B\)–\(C_k\) coincidence next to it, so the direction of any bias (section 5, E1, step 2) is visible.

Pass. Both partial rates exceed their null 95th percentile, Holm over the two.

Kill rule (decided now). If either fails: record "common-shock reading not ruled out", keep ECA as a screening tool only for this pair, and build nothing further on unwind → fails. If both fail, the 11-17 headline is downgraded on the card.

Descriptive only (no pass/fail). Conditional ECA with \(C\) required (CoinCalc CC.ceca.ts), and the Hawkes-bridge \(\hat\Phi\) with a moving-block bootstrap interval.

Data: public aggregates only (NY Fed fails, OFR repo, CFTC TFF, FRED). Descriptive, with no trading signals.
