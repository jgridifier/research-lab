Well established as a linear lead-lag measure; contested as proof of causal direction

(a) Robust in its home field

  • Origin and claims. Nolte et al. (2008, PRL 100:234101; arXiv:0712.2352, abstract and definition read) propose PSI as (a) insensitive to mixtures of independent sources, (b) meaningful for non-linear phase spectra, (c) properly weighted over frequencies. Their simulations: for truly unidirectional flow without noise PSI detects direction about as well as Granger causality; for random mixtures of independent sources Granger yields highly significant false results and PSI does not. An EEG application on 88 subjects (eyes closed) shows a front-to-back flow. The search tool lists about 594 citations.
  • Independent assessment. Haufe, Nikulin, Müller & Nolte (2013, NeuroImage; abstract read): in a simulation study PSI performs well under volume conduction, while Granger measures with standard significance testing produce spurious connectivity; a time-reversed-data surrogate makes Granger measures work about as well as PSI. (The authors overlap with the PSI paper.)
  • Failure modes and critiques. Nolte et al. 2010 (PMLR v6, abstract read): PSI, being linear, can miss nonlinear interactions. A 2022 review (Front. Neuroinformatics/PMC, snippet read): under two-way interaction PSI fails to describe direction. A 2024 paper (PubMed 38631615, abstract read): lead/lag from PSI is not generally equivalent to causal direction in nonlinear systems.
  • Maintenance. Implemented in mne-connectivity (phase_slope_index, v0.9.0, BSD-3, actively maintained, read its source) and the authors' MATLAB code (TU Berlin causality page). EEG/MEG use for over a decade.
  • Our reproduction. Planted-delay and mixture simulations reproduce the direction of the paper's claims (PSI 100% correct on coupled; false significance 3% vs Granger 10% on mixtures), but not the size of the Granger failure. Parity with mne-connectivity was not achieved (see On our data).

Verdict: well established for what it measures (a linear lead-lag direction, robust to instantaneous mixing); contested as "causality". Reason: independent simulation support and long use, with documented limits.

(b) Transfer with new results

  • Assumptions that break: few segments (6–15, not hundreds of epochs); series that are persistent and heavy-tailed; shared shocks that are real economic drivers rather than measurement mixing.
  • A result the standard toolkit gets wrong: on matched-persistence independent pairs shaped like weekly fails and DVP, the standard VAR Granger test rejects in either direction 13.4% of the time (nominal 5% per direction); PSI 0.6%. The two Granger "links" in the real data (p = 0.023, 0.042) sit inside that false-alarm range.
  • Further fields it could carry to: climate teleconnections (lead-lag between indices), hydrology (rainfall-runoff delay with shared storms), macro nowcasting with a common factor, ecology (predator-prey delays).
