Well established (the computation is exact combinatorics; the applied validation literature is small)

What the home field has established

  • Origin and lineage. Ranking without weights by dominance and Hasse diagrams comes from environmental chemistry and QSAR (Brüggemann and co-authors; Springer book Ranking and Prioritization for Multi-indicator Systems: Introduction to Partial Order Applications — found via search, not read). Averaged ranks over linear extensions and the local partial-order approximation were published in J. Chem. Inf. Comput. Sci. 44(2):618 ("Estimation of averaged ranks by a local partial order model"; search snippet only). An open review of partial-order ranking alongside QSAR is at PMC2680638 (found, not read in full).
  • Network-science version. Schoch (2018, Social Networks 54:50–60; abstract and introduction read) shows many centrality indices preserve the neighbourhood-inclusion partial order, so each index is one linear extension, and proposes rank intervals, relative rank probabilities and expected ranks instead of picking an index. The search index lists 20 citations: a niche result.
  • Algorithm. Exact rank probabilities via the lattice of ideals (De Loof, De Meyer & De Baets 2006, Fundamenta Informaticae 71:309–321; De Loof's 2009 Ghent PhD thesis), cited by netrankr; not read. Counting linear extensions is #P-complete in general, so exactness depends on the poset having few ideals; netrankr's benchmark vignette (read) gives "under about 30 elements always safe" and documents MCMC (Bubley–Dyer) and approximations with their errors on 3,000 random partial rankings.
  • Software. netrankr 2.0.0 on CRAN (David Schoch; C++ via Rcpp; pkgdown site with reference pages and four vignettes; GitHub schochastics/netrankr). Maintained.
  • Critiques and failure modes. The uniform measure over linear extensions is a convention, not a model; dominance is sensitive to small, noisy differences (the chemometrics literature studies this: "On the influence of data noise and uncertainty on ordering of objects", J. Chemometrics, doi 10.1002/cem.2764 — abstract via search only); with many indicators almost every pair becomes incomparable.

Our reproduction

  • netrankr vignette (dbces11 graph): 739,200 rankings; expected ranks 1.714, 3.000, 4.286, 7.500, 8.143, 6.857, 6.857, 7.500, 6.000, 6.000, 8.143 — identical to the printed output. Rank intervals G [2, 11] and K [3, 11] as printed.
  • Independent Python port (lattice DP) vs netrankr and brute-force enumeration: counts identical; rank probabilities within 5×10−16 on 21 small posets; expected ranks within 1.4×10−13 on the 13 real stress-test posets. netrankr ran each year in 0.005–0.22 s.
