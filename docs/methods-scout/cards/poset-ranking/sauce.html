A tolerance-aware lattice engine, netrankr as oracle

Our Python lattice-of-ideals code already matches netrankr to 10−13; netrankr (C++) is faster on these sizes. The engineering value is reach, not raw speed: a Rust core with exact big-integer counts, tolerance dominance built in (avenue 2), and width-aware ordering so 60–100 firms (all CCAR participants plus IHCs over time) or a few indicators more stay exact.

Math sketch. Cost is \(O(|\mathcal I|\cdot n)\); by Dilworth, \(|\mathcal I|\le\prod_{c}(|c|+1)\) over a chain cover, so it is polynomial for fixed width \(w\): \(|\mathcal I|\le (n/w+1)^w\). Processing ideals level by level keeps memory to two levels. For width beyond about 15, fall back to Bubley–Dyer MCMC with the exact engine as a check on sub-posets.

Why it matters here. Tolerance dominance (avenue 2) increases incomparability and therefore the number of ideals; Q2-sized program books need the approximate path anyway.

Closest prior work: netrankr's C++ exact_rank_prob and mcmc_rank_prob (Schoch); De Loof et al. (2006), cited by netrankr, not read.

Test (pass bar set now). Rank probabilities within 10−12 of netrankr on all 13 years and 20 random posets; a width-12, n = 60 poset exact in under 1 s on the box; MCMC agrees with exact within 0.01 expected rank on the 13 years.

2 · Math · Effort M · ★ Top pick
Noise-aware dominance and rank probabilities

Order two firms only when one is worse by more than projection noise on every indicator; average rank probabilities over that uncertainty instead of treating 0.1 pp as decisive.

Math sketch. Replace \(\preceq\) with \(u\preceq_\varepsilon v\iff x_{vj}-x_{uj}\ge\varepsilon_j\ \forall j\) (an interval order on each indicator, so \(\preceq_\varepsilon\) is still a partial order; transitivity holds because margins add). Choose \(\varepsilon_j\) from the year-to-year standard deviation of firm-level changes in indicator \(j\) for firms whose business mix did not change. Then a "probabilistic dominance" version: with \(x_u\sim N(\hat x_u,\Sigma)\), \(P(u\prec v)\) by simulation, and rank probabilities \(\mathbb E_{x}[P(\mathrm{rk}(u)=k\mid\preceq_x)]\) by drawing posets and averaging exact results. Concordance tests use only pairs with \(P(u\prec v)\ge 0.9\).

Why it matters here. 26% of dominance relations dissolve the next year and 2% reverse; some are surely noise. If the 72% concordance is driven by robust pairs, it should rise; if it is driven by hair-thin differences, it will fall.

Closest prior work: noise and uncertainty in partial-order ranking, J. Chemometrics, doi 10.1002/cem.2764 (search snippet: a "stability range" of noise over which the Hasse diagram is unchanged); a fuzzy posetic toolbox, Annals of Operations Research (2024), doi 10.1007/s10479-024-06352-3 (search only). Neither read; no application to stress tests found.

Test (pass bar set now). On the 13 years: year-to-year kept share of dominance relations rises from 0.74 to ≥ 0.85 while ≥ 50% of the original ordered pairs remain ordered; payout concordance on robust pairs ≥ 0.70 (within-year shuffle p < 0.01). Null: the same procedure with ε applied to indicator values shuffled across firms within year.

3 · New use on our data · Effort S
Trade-off resolution for the payout mix (Q3.5)

Score trade-off pairs indicator by indicator, separately for dividends/RWA and net buybacks/RWA, before and after the 2020 SCB regime.

Math sketch. \(A_j^{(m,r)}\) = share of trade-off pairs in regime \(r\in\{2013\text{–}19,\ 2020\text{–}25\}\) where the firm worse on indicator \(j\) has the lower payout component \(m\in\{\text{div},\text{bb}\}\). Contrast \(\Delta_j=A_j^{(\text{div},r)}-A_j^{(\text{bb},r)}\); within-year shuffle that moves dividends and buybacks together (keeps their firm-level link).

Why it matters here. The 11-16 PIM run suggested the drawdown link moved from dividends to buybacks after 2020; trade-off pairs can say which indicator each payout channel follows without choosing weights.

Closest prior work: none found for stress-test payouts; method is A1b of this card.

Test (pass bar set now). At least one indicator with \(|\Delta_j|\ge 0.08\) and Holm p < 0.05 over 3 indicators × 2 regimes; otherwise reported as "payout mix follows the same indicator as the total".
