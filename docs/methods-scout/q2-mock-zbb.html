What the Q2 mock budget datasets are doing

Two synthetic program-level zero-based-budgeting datasets, built so methods for Q2 can be tried before any real program-level budget exists. They are modeled on publicly reported totals for Corning Incorporated and Morgan Stanley. They are not either firm’s actual budgets, program lists, or headcount files. This page walks through the generator, the files, the planted answer key, the public sources, and the self-checks.

The short version

  • What it is. A pair of made-up budgeting workbooks that look like a zero-based-budgeting exercise: hundreds to a few thousand programs, a free-text request narrative plus tabular labels, a short noisy budget-versus-actual history, programs that start and stop, and three decision packages for the FY2026 cycle. They exist so Q2 methods can be scored against a known truth. Real program-level budgets are still missing.
  • What it is not. It is not an internal file from either company. No private or internal data was used. Public 10-Ks and financial supplements informed the totals (SG&A, RD&E, segment non-interest expense, employee counts). The programs themselves, their descriptions, and their year-by-year dollars are generated.
  • How it was built. The script generate_q2_mock_zbb.py (seed 20261113) instantiates programs from archetypes at publicly known sites, plants pooling families and hidden clusters, writes a noiseless true path, draws noisy budgets and actuals, then rakes those dollars to the public totals. Descriptions are stitched from the stated labels only. The answer key is written last, in a separate file.
  • How to use it. Fit methods on programs.csv, history.csv and decision_packages.csv. Score them with ground_truth.json. Do not use the answer key as a feature file — that would leak the answers to Q2.1–Q2.4. The first methods run on these files is the 13 November scout (ebnm and DenseLines).
  • Where the files live. Drive folder Q2 mock ZBB datasets (synthetic, 2026-11-13). This page does not republish the CSVs.

01

What this is, and why it exists

Q2 is a standing research question, added and refined on 13 November 2026. Jared’s original wording, kept verbatim in QUESTIONS.md, asked what is interesting to learn when you have a lot of free-text descriptions, turn them into classifications and implied features, and then face a budgeting use case: hundreds or thousands of programs, each with its own noise and not much history. He then approved a refined statement, all five sub-questions, and the addition of tabular data alongside the text.

Refined Q2. Suppose we have hundreds to thousands of programs. Each one has free-text descriptions plus tabular data, which we turn into categories and implied features, and each has only a few noisy periods of numbers. Past tracking trends and category mix over time, what can we reliably learn about each program and about the portfolio as a whole?

  1. Borrowing strength. If we pool similar programs, how much better do we estimate each one’s real level and trend than by looking at it alone, given how short its history is?
  2. Text versus numbers. Which programs’ spending doesn’t match what their descriptions say, and which ones quietly change category over time?
  3. Real change or noise. When a total moves, how much comes from the program mix changing, how much from programs actually behaving differently, and how much is just noise?
  4. Structure. Do programs form groups that rise and fall together, or trade off against each other, in ways the categories don’t show?
  5. Seeing it. How can one chart show a thousand short, noisy series without turning into spaghetti?

The mocks were built the same day so those sub-questions can be tried on something that has the right shape: a manufacturer-like firm and a financial-firm-like firm, internet-informed at the total level, with a hidden answer key. Status is in progress. Real program-level budgets, actuals, headcount and decision packages for any firm are still missing.

How the generator works

Everything below is what generate_q2_mock_zbb.py actually does when run with seed 20261113. Regenerate with python3 generate_q2_mock_zbb.py --out <dir>. The same script is stored in the Drive folder next to the outputs.

  1. Program hierarchy and taxonomy. Each firm is built from archetypes — plants, desks, functions — at publicly known cities and sites. The assignment of a given program to a site is synthetic. Archetype counts are scaled into the “hundreds to a few thousand” range (×1.30 for the Corning mock, ×2.00 for the Morgan Stanley mock). Identifiers look like GLW-OC-0001 or MS-IS-0001.
    • Corning mock segments follow the 2025 official reporting lines, including Automotive (Environmental Technologies and Automotive Glass Solutions, combined 1 January 2025) and Display (renamed from Display Technologies), plus a Corporate bucket: Optical Communications (349 programs), Corporate (161), Specialty Materials (128), Automotive (128), Display (123), Life Sciences (99), Hemlock and Emerging Growth Businesses (91).
    • Morgan Stanley mock segments are Institutional Securities (648), Wealth Management (520), Firm Functions (384) and Investment Management (288). Firm Functions is visible in the mock; public filings fold those costs into the three business segments. Each Firm Functions program therefore also has a reported_segment (IS, WM or IM), drawn with weights proportional to 2025 segment NIE ($21,541m / $22,414m / $5,047m), and that field is what the rake uses.
    • Each program also has a subsegment, an operational function, a ZBB bucket (Corning: sga, rde, plant_indirect; Morgan Stanley: compensation, noncomp), and, for MS non-comp, one of the official category labels: brokerage_clearing, info_processing, professional, occupancy, marketing, other.
    • Some archetypes share a family_id (pooling family). Some carry a latent cluster_id. A few off-archetype “surprise” members are added to each cluster so a cluster is not just a restatement of the subsegment label, and those surprise members never mention the common driver in the text.
  2. Entry and exit, so histories are short and unbalanced. Growth archetypes (for example AI capacity, digital wealth, alternatives) often start in 2022–2025. Declining archetypes (idle display, some automotive substrate, branch occupancy) exit with probability 0.35. Independently, about 10% of ordinary programs start late and about 7% exit early. Years outside [start_year, end_year] are omitted from history.csv, not filled with zeros. In the Corning mock, 778 of 1,079 programs have all six years; 27 have only 2025. In the Morgan Stanley mock, 1,404 of 1,840 have all five years; 75 have only 2025. That short, start-and-stop panel is the Q2.5 visualization problem.
  3. Stated labels, true labels, and the free-text description. Each program is born with cost type, discretionary flag, intent and driver. Those begin as the true labels. Then a planted mismatch step (about 6.5% of active programs; at least 40) rewrites the stated labels for five mismatch types: stated mandatory / actually discretionary; stated transform / actually run; stated growth / actually declining; stated people / actually vendor; stated run / actually transform. A further drift step (about 4% of active programs; at least 28) changes a true field in 2023 or 2024 — or later, if the program had not started yet — while the description is written to lag, still speaking in the old language. The narrative itself is a stitched budget-request paragraph: opener, scope, people, a cost-type beat, optional cluster language only when text_reveals_cluster is true (a coin flip at probability 0.55, except surprise members, who never reveal), mismatch color language or a why-this-spend beat, a history clause, a risk, and a close. It uses stated labels only. There are no true_*, cluster, family or mismatch columns in programs.csv.
  4. History, noise, and hidden structure. Members of a pooling family are re-anchored around a shared 2025 family mean with a between-program log-sigma of 0.07, then given generous observation noise (σ = 0.18, or 0.085 if the program is also in a cluster) so that short-history pooling should beat estimating each program alone. The true path is walked back from that 2025 run-rate with a mild generic drift of about 2% a year, times a latent cluster factor 1 + loading × F_t. “Stated growth, actually declining” programs also get a −11% idiosyncratic decline near the end of the window. True dollars are floored at $25,000. Actuals are a mean-one lognormal around true: actual = true × exp(N(−0.5σ², σ)), with a base σ of about 0.065–0.15 depending on size and the discretionary flag. Budgets use a slight conservative bias (β = −0.02) and smaller noise (η = 0.70σ). Headcount roughly tracks the true path, with less noise, and is raked later to the official employee count. Contractor FTE is entirely synthetic.
  5. Raking to public totals. After the noisy draws, each year’s actuals are scaled so that published totals are hit. The same factor is applied to the true dollars and the budget, which preserves actual-to-true ratios and slightly reshapes year totals. FTE is raked separately to the official employee count (full- and part-time combined; the mock does not split part-time).
    • Corning mock, every year 2020–2025: SG&A and RD&E actuals match the 10-K exactly. Plant-indirect is scaled to 20% of that year’s GAAP cost of sales — a documented assumption, not a disclosed split.
    • Morgan Stanley mock, 2022–2025: compensation actuals are one-way raked to official segment compensation. Non-comp actuals are raked by iterative proportional fitting to official segment non-comp and official firm-level category shares. Category dollar targets are scaled so they sum to the segment-gross non-comp total (official firm categories plus that year’s intersegment elimination). 2021 is firm-level and category-level only; 2021 segment subtotals are not rake targets.
  6. FY2026 decision packages. Every active program gets three rows, built off 2025 actuals: a minimum (84–92% of actual if the program is stated mandatory, otherwise 70–84%), a current (98–105%), and an enhanced (112–132% if stated grow or transform, otherwise 107–118%). Requested FTE is 0.82×, 1.0× and 1.10× of 2025 FTE. Justifications are short template paragraphs. The approval outcome is planted, not a real committee: mismatch programs of the first three types are cut more often; growth or transform members of the AI, digital and wealth-fee clusters are enhanced more often; stated-mandatory programs are usually approved at current. approval_outcome and approved_usd are repeated on all three level rows because they are program-level decisions.

The answer key records family_and_cluster_observation_sigma as 0.095. The generator code itself sets cluster-member observation σ to 0.085 (and family-only members to 0.18). Treat 0.085 as the value that was actually drawn.

The planted answer key, and how to test each Q2 sub-question

ground_truth.json is the score sheet. A method that reads it on the way in is cheating. The block below is what is planted, and a fair way to test against it.

Q2.1Borrowing strength

What’s planted. Pooling families of similar programs that share a true 2025 mean, with tight between-program dispersion (designed log-sigma 0.07) and generous observation noise, plus short histories. Corning mock: 36 families, 717 member programs, family sizes 10–42. Morgan Stanley mock: 40 families, 1,440 members, sizes 16–96. Not every program is in a family.

How to test. Estimate each program’s 2025 level (or trend) from its own short noisy history, then again with shrinkage or partial pooling toward the family — or toward an observable grouping such as segment × function, if you are not allowed to peek at family_id. Score both against true_levels_by_program_year. A built-in James–Stein check in the answer key already shrinks 2025 actuals 45% of the way to the family mean of actuals and compares MSE to 2025 true levels: mean improvement 0.580 on the Corning mock and 0.407 on the Morgan Stanley mock, so pooling is designed to help. The 13 November scout found the same direction with ebnm.

Q2.2Text versus numbers

What’s planted. Programs whose stated (description) labels disagree with their true labels or spending path, and programs whose true category drifts while the description lags.

Drift fields are intent, cost type or discretionary. Most drift years are 2023 or 2024; a few late-starting programs drift in 2025 or 2026 (the planter bumps the year to start_year + 1 when needed). The standing true_* record is the terminal, post-drift value; the drift block has the path.

How to test. From descriptions and histories alone, flag mismatches and dating of category change. Score against mismatch_text_vs_numbers and category_drift. “Stated growth, actually declining” is the cleanest path-versus-text test, because those programs also have a declining true path.

Q2.3Real change or noise

What’s planted. A documented 2024→2025 shift-share, computed after raking. Behavior is the change in continuing programs. Mix is entrant spend in 2025 minus exiter spend in 2024. On true (noiseless) levels, behavior + mix equals the total change. On actuals, residual = actual change − true change, which equals the change in the noise draws.

The 13 November scout found that portfolio-level noise really is only about 2% (Corning) to well under 1% (Morgan Stanley) of the change, so most of the work is separating mix from behavior, not subtracting noise.

Q2.4Hidden structure

What’s planted. Three latent clusters per firm. The model is true_t *= (1 + loading × F_t), then idiosyncratic log-noise, then raking. About half the members mention the common driver in the description (text_reveals_cluster); the rest, including all surprise members, do not. Clusters are not identical to segment or stated-category labels.

How to test. Recover co-moving or trade-off groups from the histories, with or without the text, and score against latent_clusters. A recoverability diagnostic in the answer key reports year-on-year actual-percent-change correlations. Same-sign pairs sit clearly above a random-pair control (Corning random-pair mean 0.12; AI-demand same-sign mean 0.36; auto aftertreatment 0.68. Morgan Stanley random-pair mean 0.02; wealth-fee 0.30; branch-versus-digital same-sign 0.34). The design note says opposite-sign pairs on a tradeoff cluster should be negative. In the realized actuals they were not: Display/Specialty cross-sign mean +0.15, branch-versus-digital cross-sign mean +0.12. Noise and raking have made the tradeoff harder to see in actual year-on-year changes than the co-moving groups. That is a property of this seed, not a reason to ignore the planted loadings.

Q2.5Seeing it

What’s planted. The panel itself: about a thousand (Corning) or eighteen hundred (Morgan Stanley) short, differently scaled, start-and-stop series. That is the visualization problem. There is no extra hidden label for Q2.5; a method works if a reader can see structure — including the Q2.4 clusters — without spaghetti.

How to test. Chart the histories (log levels, residuals, densities) and ask whether a hidden co-moving group is visible, especially the members whose text does not mention the driver. The 13 November scout used DenseLines for this and had to make the method gap-safe, because programs that start or stop made the reference implementation return NaN in every column.

Assumptions and limitations

These are the documented gaps from each firm’s self_check.md, plus two generator notes a reviewer should know.

  • It is a mock. Segment spend splits that the companies do not publish are synthetic. Descriptions are generated and will have a family resemblance no real budget office would produce at this scale. Do not present these files as actual firm data.
  • Plant-indirect is an assumption. 20% of GAAP cost of sales. Corning does not disclose manufacturing overhead separately. Direct materials and most direct labor are outside the ZBB universe, so official COGS is much larger than plant-indirect actuals.
  • SG&A and RD&E are not disclosed by segment. Segment program spend in the Corning mock is a synthetic allocation informed by segment sales and by the known RD&E intensity of Specialty Materials and Optical Communications. It is not an official split. 2023 Automotive / Life Sciences / Hemlock restated sales were not all separately available in the sources used, so they are not rake targets.
  • Capex is not a ZBB-universe total ($1,282m in 2025, $965m in 2024). Some programs are flagged capex-like (furnace campaigns, capacity) but their dollars live in plant-indirect or SG&A and will not roll to official capex.
  • The 2025 Corning employee count (67,200) is a large increase from 2024 (56,300). The mock follows the official path; there was no public explanation to plant into program-level hiring beyond Optical Communications growth.
  • Intersegment eliminations are a documented gap, not a program. The Morgan Stanley mock has no negative “elimination” rows. The sum of IS + WM + IM NIE exceeds firm NIE by the official item ($660m in 2025, $570m in 2024, $520m in 2023, $501m in 2022). Firm-level non-comp category shares match the supplement; category dollars are scaled to the segment-gross non-comp total, so they also overshoot official firm categories by that elimination.
  • 2021 Morgan Stanley segment totals are not matched. That year is firm-level and category-level only. Do not treat 2021 segment subtotals as matching a filing.
  • Financial-advisor headcount is not disclosed in the 2025 10-K or 4Q25 supplement. FA Compensation programs are a synthetic disaggregation of official WM compensation, not a count of advisors.
  • The March 2025 workforce action (severance $144m, about 2% of staff) is cited in the 10-K but is not modelled as a one-off program; it is absorbed into the 2025 compensation totals.
  • Brokerage, clearing and exchange fees are volume-driven and mostly mandatory. They are in the ZBB universe because they are official NIE, but they are not a classic decision package in the same sense as a discretionary desk.
  • FTE and contractors. FTE is raked to the official employee count, which includes full- and part-time staff. The mock does not distinguish part-time. Contractor FTE is entirely synthetic.
  • Two generator notes. The answer key lists cluster-member observation σ as 0.095; the code draws 0.085. And the realized year-on-year correlations of actuals for opposite-sign members of the two tradeoff clusters came out slightly positive, so a method that looks only at actual changes will find the tradeoffs weaker than the planted loadings suggest.
